AI is changing Malaysian jobs faster than training and skills lists can keep up. In Malaysia, 56% of professionals use AI tools at work, but only 26% have had formal training (Hays Asia Salary Guide 2025, via the Domain 1 brief). In October 2024, the government said about 600,000 workers in 10 sectors needed reskilling within three to five years (Bernama). TalentCorp projects about 620,000 workers, 18% of the workforce in the sectors it studied, will be significantly affected (Impact Study). A World Bank and ISIS Malaysia study rates 28% of the workforce highly exposed to AI (World Bank, 2025). Skills are also under-used: in Q2 2026, 35.2% of employed tertiary-educated workers held semi-skilled or low-skilled jobs (OpenDOSM; hiring-bar evidence).

Our user is a working adult in Malaysia, in any industry, who wonders what AI means for their job. They come with a worry and a few minutes, often on a phone. Some want to keep their job safe, some to move to a role they have in mind, and some to learn where they could go (users).

Today they get static lists. TalentCorp's MyMahir maps roles and skills, but shows no score, time estimate, scenario or SDG tag, and no Malaysian tool we checked joins role data to a scored gap and a timed pathway (prior art). Without dated evidence, a worker cannot tell which skills to learn first, and may spend scarce hours on tasks AI is taking over.

Kotak, Malay for "box", is a web app. Kopi, a cat counsellor, asks 12 can-do questions, rated 1 to 4, about the user's job or a role they want. It draws a Future Skills Radar of the job's 6 core skills, ranks matched roles and builds a dated roadmap in about 3 minutes.

The six must-haves:

  1. Skills profile intake: situation, study, a job title in any language, a goal (stay, move, explore or not sure) and the 12 questions.
  2. AI-trend signal layer: dated Anthropic Economic Index snapshots for Malaysia (April and May 2026) and DOSM Job Market Insights, refreshed weekly.
  3. Future-role matching: ranked roles, each with a fit score, its maths and named gaps.
  4. Reskilling pathway: ordered steps, each with hours and one free resource, and a finish date set by the user's weekly hours.
  5. SDG mapping: skills and jobs carry SDG targets, and the roadmap highlights steps by goal.
  6. Handling uncertainty: targets are labelled estimates, Split shows slow and fast AI-uptake futures, and Unknown marks thin data.

Fixed rules, not AI, set every verdict, score, ranking and roadmap order. AI does four jobs:

  • Before the demo, a RunPod GPU batch groups each O*NET job's own tasks into 6 skills and drafts the questions. A validator rejects any task outside that job.
  • Live, Claude Haiku 5.5 explains each verdict with its dated sources and matches Malay and local job titles.
  • Ask Kopi, a chat agent, answers through 8 read-only tools over the same rules, which other assistants reach through an MCP server.
  • ElevenLabs voices Kopi, with mute and a text fallback.

From the Phase 1 workshops, we use GenAI APIs (Claude), Postgres (Neon), cloud hosting (Cloudflare Workers and Replit) and RunPod serverless GPUs (workshops; Workshop 1).

Kotak is a TypeScript monorepo run with Bun. It has two apps, apps/web (the React app) and apps/api (the Hono API), and four packages: shared (the rules and Zod schemas), sdk (the check-up engine), agent (the AI tools) and browser (the JobStreet scraper's browser).

  • Frontend: React and Vite, Tailwind CSS, Recharts for the radar and the roadmap charts, Lucide icons, and pixi-live2d-display for Kopi, built on Live2D's Hijiki sample model. It has phone (390 px) and desktop (1440 px) layouts, follows WCAG 2.2 AA and has a reduced-motion mode (design system).
  • Rule engine in the browser: the app loads precomputed, dated JSON snapshots and computes levels, targets, verdicts, fit scores and roadmap order in the browser. Switching the snapshot from April to May 2026 recomputes them at once. A guest session in localStorage keeps answers, goals and ticked steps.
  • API: Hono, on Cloudflare Workers for the main link and on Bun for Replit. One server serves the React build and the API. Its routes match job titles, write cited explanations, proxy text to speech, run Ask Kopi and serve an MCP endpoint. Zod validates every request and response. On Workers, the MCP and chat routes run in a Durable Object.
  • GenAI: the Claude API with Claude Haiku 5.5. The API caches each answer by a SHA-256 hash of the request and pre-warms the cache for the demo roles. A check rejects any explanation that changes a verdict or lacks a dated citation, and falls back to a fixed template.
(continued)
  • Agent layer: @kotak/agent exposes 8 read-only tools over @kotak/sdk: find a role, get the questions, suggest roles, run a check-up, what changed, national skill gaps, job ads in Malaysia and list sources. Ask Kopi calls them through Claude, and outside assistants call them through /api/mcp. Every number the agent quotes comes from a tool.
  • Voice: ElevenLabs. Fixed lines are pre-generated with eleven_v4, and live lines use eleven_v4_turbo. The API caches audio by a hash of the text, and the key stays on the server (TTS notes).
  • Data pipeline: Bun scripts ingest O*NET 30.1, filter the Economic Index to Malaysia, Singapore and global rows, extract DOSM Job Market Insights (Q3 2019 to Q2 2026), and map fields of study to jobs through the NCES CIP to SOC crosswalk. Every record carries its source, date and layer. A GitHub Actions workflow checks for new releases every Saturday and rebuilds the snapshots.
  • Database: Neon Postgres 18 (region aws-ap-southeast-1). One table holds the anonymous national view: only a role code, 6 skill levels, a sample flag and a date, with totals only for groups of 10 or more; no screen sends check-ups yet, so it holds labelled sample rows. Other tables hold the scraper's JobStreet listings, the admins' reviews and matched skills, and the admin accounts.
  • GPU: a RunPod serverless endpoint runs Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 on vLLM for the one-time batch that builds 6 skills and 18 can-do statements for each of 923 jobs.
  • Hosting and CI: GitHub Actions type-checks, builds and deploys Kotak to Cloudflare Workers on every merge to main, or when a member clicks "Run workflow" (deploy notes). Replit publishes the same main branch from the team's own account. API keys and the database URL live in GitHub, Cloudflare and Replit secrets, never in code.

  • Empathise: on 21 October, we named three kinds of worker: stay strong, move to a target role, or ask where to go (users). Research showed Malaysian graduates find work, often below their skill level (hiring-bar evidence).
  • Define: one user and one moment: a worried worker with a few minutes, who leaves with a plan to start this week.
  • Ideate: we gave five AI models the brief, as other teams would, and 17 of their 75 ideas served this same worker (field simulation). So Kotak's uniqueness comes from its mechanism: rated can-do questions, skills built from the job's own tasks, and verdicts set by visible rules.
  • Prototype and test: six AI-played experts raised 43 attacks on the first Kotak (roast). We cut the planted-mistake test and moved the demo from a sample card to real data. We set the visual design in Figma before coding.
  • Deploy: the live demo swaps the Economic Index from April to May 2026, and the radar, ranking and roadmap move. Cached Claude answers and a fixed-template fallback cover API overload. GitHub Actions deploys to Cloudflare Workers on every merge to main, so any member can ship.

Target 4.4, "By 2030, substantially increase the number of youth and adults who have relevant skills, including technical and vocational skills, for employment, decent jobs and entrepreneurship". It is measured by indicator 4.4.1, "Proportion of youth and adults with information and communications technology (ICT) skills, by type of skill" (UN SDG API, goal 4).

The target, indicator and baseline come from the UN SDG API, accessed on 22 October 2026, following our impact notes.

In 2024, 39.8% of Malaysians aged 15 and over had above-basic digital skills in all 5 skill areas, and 19.6% reported writing a computer program in a programming language. Both figures are indicator 4.4.1, series SE_ADT_ACTS, from DOSM (UN SDG API, Malaysia). For AI skills, 56% of Malaysian professionals use AI tools at work, but only 26% have had formal training (Domain 1 brief).

The sourced-roadmap rate. It is the percentage of finished check-ups that reach a roadmap in under 3 minutes, where every verdict, fit score and milestone shows its source and date. A second count tracks uncited recommendations, which must stay at 0 (LLM credibility notes).

100% of 20 seeded test profiles reach a sourced roadmap in under 3 minutes with 0 uncited recommendations (LLM credibility notes). On 100% of the hand-checked demo roles, the April-to-May 2026 snapshot swap changes at least one verdict (demo roles).
