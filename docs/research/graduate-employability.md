# Graduate employability in Malaysian public universities

This note answers whether graduate employability gives Malaysian public university career centres an adoption incentive, how MOHE measures it, and what current policy and results show.

## Summary

- Yes. MOHE made graduate employability a higher education system aspiration and its 2021–2025 strategy named it a senior-management KPI, with a target above 80% by 2025. [1][2]
- The latest MOHE figures cover 2025: 92.3% overall, 94.7% for public university graduates, and 92.5% for the broad ICT field. [7]
- SKPG is an annual online survey answered by graduates around convocation. Since 2024, the headline rate measures employed graduates as a share of graduates in the labour force. [3][4][6]
- The headline rate can count gig work and unpaid family work after just one hour in the survey week. It does not establish a graduate’s pay, job security, or match to skill level. [5][6][15]
- There are practical institutional incentives: SETARA includes an employed-graduate indicator, MQA accreditation reviews require employability analysis, and MOHE publishes institution-level data. A public-university KPI page also links employability to a funding-formula framework, but does not state a current weight. [8][9][10][12]
- New plans cover 2026–2030 and 2026–2035. They stress qualification-matched employment, pay, AI and digital skills. The full 2026–2030 plan text was not found, so its detailed career-centre requirements remain unknown. [13][14]

## Findings

### 1. Graduate employability as a KPI

Yes. The Ministry of Higher Education (MOHE, now KPT in Malay) set a system-level aspiration in the Malaysia Education Blueprint 2015–2025 (Higher Education): “On quality of graduates, the Ministry aspires to increase the current 75% graduate employability rate to more than 80% in 2025.” The target was **more than 80% by 2025**. This was an aspiration in the blueprint, not evidence of a current 2026 target. [1]

KPT’s Graduate Employability Strategic Plan 2021–2025 made the institutional link more explicit. It calls graduate employability a key performance indicator for higher education institution senior management and sets an outcome of “Kadar GE >80%” (GE rate above 80%). It also names career centres as partners in student career development. [2]

### 2. How SKPG measures it

MOHE describes the Graduate Tracer Study (Kajian Pengesanan Graduan, SKPG) as a yearly online survey of graduates. It asks graduates who completed their studies about their employment status and destination, as well as their views on their programme and institution. SKPG 1 covers public universities, private higher education institutions and other non-TVET institutions. MOHE’s 2025 statistics define respondents as students who passed and completed their studies and joined the survey; the report says SKPG results are reported during convocation. [3][4]

The current SKPG questionnaire counts a person as working if they worked full-time or part-time for at least one hour during the survey week for wages, profit or family gain. It includes employees, employers, self-employed or freelance workers, and unpaid family workers. The page also names gig work, housemanship and paid chambering as work. [5]

From 2024, MOHE reports the **Graduate Employment Rate**: employed graduates divided by graduates in the labour force. Graduates outside the labour force, including those studying further, upskilling or waiting for placement, are excluded. The older Graduate Employability rate included graduates working, studying further, training or upskilling, and waiting for placement within six months. The break means the two rates should not be read as one continuous series. [3][6]

The headline rate is a short-term employment status measure. Its definition has no earnings threshold, minimum hours beyond the one-hour reference-week rule, job-security test or required match between the job and the graduate’s qualification. Thus it does not show whether a graduate is underemployed or working below their skill level. DOSM publishes a separate skills-related underemployment measure for employed people with tertiary education who work in semi-skilled or low-skilled occupations. [5][6][15]

### 3. Latest published national and ICT figures

The latest MOHE annual tracer-study tables located are for **2025**, published on 14 May 2026. They report Malaysian-citizen graduates. The national Graduate Employment Rate was **92.3%**; for public universities, it was **94.7%**. [7]

For the broad Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) field, the 2025 table reports **92.5%** employed: 15,767 of 17,043 graduates. This is a field-level ICT figure, not a separate rate for computer science, computing or IT programmes. [7]

### 4. Effects on universities

**Funding and KPIs.** USM’s KPI Management System page says MOHE’s public-university funding formula focuses on outcomes and lists “Graduates Employability” as one of seven headline KPIs. This is evidence of a funding-policy and management link. The page does not provide a current formula, employability weight, institution-specific payment or penalty. [9]

**Ratings.** The SETARA 2022 instrument includes “Percentage of Bachelor graduates being employed based on the Tracer Study” under “Quality of Graduates.” It uses completed SKPG cycles within six months of graduation. This makes graduate employment an input to a national higher education rating. [8]

**Accreditation.** MQA’s 2024 guidance for evaluating academic-programme standards says departments must analyse market needs and graduate employability. The guidance makes this part of compliance evaluation after a programme has produced a graduate cohort. It does not set a universal GE percentage threshold. [10]

**MyRA and public reporting.** KPT describes MyRA as a national rating for research quality and research-related performance. The MyRA material reviewed does not list GE as a criterion. MOHE’s public GE search lets users inspect results by year, qualification level, programme, institution type and institution. [11][12]

For a career centre, this gives GE a real institutional reason for adoption: senior-management KPI reporting, a SETARA indicator, accreditation evidence and public institution-level results. The sources reviewed support a funding-policy link, but do not establish how much a current university allocation changes with GE. [2][8][9][10][12]

### 5. National plans from 2025 onward

KPT’s **Graduate Employability Strategic Plan 2026–2030 (PSKG)** was launched as a national reference in August 2026. A January 2026 town-hall report says the goal is not only employment, but work matching academic qualifications and pay matching skill and expertise. The launch report also mentions reskilling, upskilling and stackable learning in response to new technology, machines and equipment. [13]

The **Malaysia Higher Education Blueprint 2026–2035** sets annual targets to be established for skills gaps and labour-market alignment, graduate earnings and wage growth, and graduate employment rates. It says graduates should have “future- and present-ready skills, including AI, digital analytics.” It also mentions career support and post-graduation pathways in a discussion of international student experience; that is not a stated career-centre mandate. [14]

Career centres do have an explicit precedent in the 2021–2025 strategy, which calls for stronger career-centre collaboration and certified career counsellors. The current PSKG launch summary does not name AI or career centres. Since the full 2026–2030 plan text was not located, its detailed provisions on either topic remain unconfirmed. [2][13]

## Unconfirmed

- No numeric graduate-employment target for the 2026–2035 blueprint was confirmed. It says annual targets will be established. The former “more than 80% in 2025” target should not be presented as current. [1][14]
- The current funding formula’s GE weight or direct effect on a public university’s allocation was not found. The USM KPI page documents the framework but not a present-day formula or consequence. [9]
- A separate 2025 course-level rate for computer science, computing or IT was not found. The published ICT figure covers a broader field. [7]
- The full PSKG 2026–2030 text was not found. The launch and town-hall reports confirm the plan and some themes, but cannot establish every initiative or whether the full plan names AI or career centres. [13]
- The sources reviewed do not establish that MyRA uses GE, or that GE has a fixed accreditation pass threshold. [10][11]
- The published headline rate does not measure skill-related underemployment. Whether every SKPG questionnaire version contains a separate job-to-qualification matching question was not confirmed. [5][6][15]
