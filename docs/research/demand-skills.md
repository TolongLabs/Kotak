# Malaysian skill-demand sources with history

This note answers which scoped sources offer repeat, dated Malaysian skill-demand evidence for a four-layer sector-to-skill engine.

Contents: [Summary](#summary) · [Sources](#sources) · [Details](#details) · [Unconfirmed](#unconfirmed) · [References](#references)

## Summary

- None of the reviewed sources provides a strong, open, comparable Malaysian time series of skill-level job demand across sectors.
- MyNSR is the closest role-to-skill source: it labels skills from job-ad data, but the displayed data ends on 31 December 2021 and covers a 68-role pilot.
- MyCOL adds repeated Malaysian employer shortage evidence at occupation level from 2015/16 to 2024/25. Its editions do not expose a stable skill-by-occupation time series.
- GitHub Innovation Graph is the most usable open history: quarterly Malaysian language and repository-topic activity under CC0. It measures developer activity, not hiring demand.
- LinkedIn, Coursera, Microsoft, AWS and WEF add dated skills snapshots or forecasts. Their Malaysian signals are limited to AI, learner behaviour, employer expectations, or broad skill groups.
- O*NET is a reusable US fallback for posting-derived software-skill flags. The project must keep source terms visible; Stack Overflow is ODbL, WEF is CC BY-NC-ND, and MyMahir terms restrict automated access and public redistribution.

## Details

### LinkedIn Skills on the Rise

LinkedIn published a global “Most In-Demand Skills for 2024” list, then country editions titled “Skills on the Rise” in 2025 and 2026. The 2024 list drew on LinkedIn member and hiring data across 200 regions and countries but published a global list, not a Malaysian one. The 2025 edition compares 2024 with 2023 and uses profile skill additions, hiring success and paid job-post presence. Its eight countries are Australia, Brazil, France, Germany, India, Spain, the UK and the US. The 2026 list covers 12 markets, including India and Singapore, and uses profile acquisition and hiring success. Neither country edition names Malaysia. [1–2, 47]

There is no public CSV or API for the rankings. LinkedIn’s robots.txt returned HTTP 200 and says automated access needs express permission; it disallows paths such as /jobs?runSearch* and /api/jobPostings/jobs*. The User Agreement, effective 3 November 2025, bars software used “to scrape or copy the Services.” Do not automate access to job listings. No open licence for the published lists was found. [45–46]

### LinkedIn Economic Graph

The 2025 Economic Graph article links a report with an annual AI-talent concentration chart for selected countries across 2016–2023. Malaysia is among the selected countries. This is member-profile AI-skill concentration, a supply-side signal. The article also reports that AI talent grew across six Southeast Asian countries through 2024, but does not provide a Malaysian job-demand series in its text. The report PDF URL, https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/Harnessing-AI.pdf, returned HTTP 200 (19,738,842 bytes). Its chart excerpt is “AI talent concentration, 2016-2023, %, selected countries.” No CSV or API was found. [3]

A 2022 LinkedIn / Asian Development Bank report covers digital-skills hiring from January 2017 to February 2020 across India, Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines. The reported annual increase is an Asia-Pacific average, not a Malaysia-specific skill count. This is a hiring proxy at skill-group level, not a series of named skills. [4]

LinkedIn reports have no open data licence identified here. The User Agreement and robots rules cited above apply.

### GitHub Innovation Graph

The dataset has eight CSVs reported quarterly since January 2020. It counts developers performing GitHub activity and suppresses economy metrics below 100 unique developers. Location uses the user’s IP-derived location. The dataset is CC0-1.0. [5]

Tested downloads:

- Languages: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/innovationgraph/main/data/languages.csv returned HTTP 200 (5,832,978 bytes). Header: num_pushers,language,language_type,iso2_code,year,quarter. First file row: 2066,HTML,markup,AE,2020,1. First Malaysia row: 4580,HTML,markup,MY,2020,1. Latest Malaysia point: 101,NSIS,programming,MY,2026,1.
- Topics: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/innovationgraph/main/data/topics.csv returned HTTP 200 (1,892,445 bytes). Header: num_pushers,topic,iso2_code,year,quarter. First Malaysia row: 452,config,MY,2021,1. Eight topic rows meet the threshold in Malaysia in 2026 Q1. [6]

These rows reflect developer activity on GitHub. They do not show vacancies, pay, hiring or demand across non-technical occupations. Treat language and topic increases as a tech-only activity signal.

### Stack Overflow Developer Survey

The annual survey asks developers which technologies they have used and want to use. Recent CSV fields include LanguageHaveWorkedWith and LanguageWantToWorkWith, plus databases, cloud platforms, web frameworks and tools. It is a developer-technology signal, not a broad labour-market survey. The official repository says respondents are self-selecting and drawn mainly through Stack Overflow channels. [7]

Malaysia respondent counts, by survey edition:

- 2011–2013: 0 Malaysia rows in each available CSV.
- 2014: 18; 2015: 70; 2016: 140; 2017: 192; 2018: 363; 2019: 299; 2020: 231.
- 2021: 333; 2022: 206; 2023: 264; 2024: 160 in the published chart; 2025: 82 in the published chart.
- 2026: exact Malaysian count unconfirmed. The public country chart shows only its top ten. The official results blog says the full dataset would be released “soon”; the tested 2026 results.csv URL returned HTTP 404. [8–14]

The 2011–2023 counts above were counted from rows in the official archived results CSVs, matching the country field to Malaysia. This is a row count, not an official reweighted estimate. The 2024 CSV has 161 Malaysia rows, while its published chart reports 160. Use the chart figure and retain the mismatch as a data-quality note. The 2025 chart reports Malaysia at 82 (0.2%). The samples are small and self-selected, so trend changes can reflect response mix and question changes. [7, 11–12]

Access test: https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/StackExchange/Survey/main/packages/archive/2023/results.csv with byte range 0–15000 returned HTTP 206. Its header includes Country and LanguageHaveWorkedWith. The 2025 file at https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/StackExchange/Survey/main/packages/archive/2025/results.csv with byte range 0–15000 returned HTTP 206. The first data row excerpt was Country=Ukraine; LanguageHaveWorkedWith=Bash/Shell (all shells);Dart;SQL; LanguageWantToWorkWith=Dart. The 2026 raw file URL, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StackExchange/Survey/main/packages/archive/2026/results.csv, returned HTTP 404 (14 bytes). [7, 13]

The survey database uses ODbL 1.0 and individual cell contents use DbCL 1.0. The repo says adapted databases must remain open under ODbL. Do not relicense the source survey database as Apache 2.0. Keep any derived database separately licensed and attributed. [7]

### Coursera Global Skills Report

Coursera’s GSR is annual from 2019 through 2026. Its figures mainly describe Coursera learner proficiency and enrolments. The 2025 Malaysia profile reports learner growth in skills Coursera labels “Top employer skills,” including AI/ML +152%. The report methodology ties these figures to Coursera course enrolment growth, so “employer” does not mean job-post demand. [14]

The 2025 PDF, https://assets.ctfassets.net/2pudprfttvy6/3ELFKTA8GBPBuRkNrOzmpS/24fc7ec2372d0adb96965340069f705c/Global_Skills_Report_2025.pdf, returned HTTP 200 (3,085,381 bytes). Its Malaysia profile is a single learner snapshot; a short excerpt is “AI/ML +152%.” The 2026 report ranks Malaysia 36th on its AI-Human Skills Synergy Index. That index combines learner data with wider national conditions, so it is not directly comparable to the 2025 learner proficiency ranks. No public row-level dataset or open report-data licence was found. Coursera’s Terms of Use were effective 1 January 2026. [14–15]

Official GSR profile figures for Malaysia in 2021–2024 were not confirmed from reachable official report sources. See Unconfirmed.

### Coursera Job Skills Report

The Job Skills Report is annual from 2022 through 2026; the 2026 edition is its fifth annual report. The latest report draws on nearly six million enterprise learners, but its published skill categories focus on Data, IT, and Software & Product Development. No Malaysian country series is published on the report page. [16–17]

It measures learning priorities and enrolment growth, not job-ad counts. No open data licence or public Malaysia extract was found. It can inform reskilling plan choices, but it cannot supply the engine’s Malaysia demand verdict.

### Microsoft Work Trend Index

Microsoft published Malaysia findings in 2024, 2025 and 2026. The 2024 edition reports that 62% of Malaysian business leaders would not hire someone without AI skills. The 2025 edition reports that 59% of Malaysian managers expect AI training or upskilling to become a core team responsibility within five years. The 2026 edition surveyed 2,000 Malaysian full-time and self-employed knowledge workers and focuses on AI use and work design. [18–20]

These are three snapshots with different questions and populations. They show employer and worker expectations around AI, not a comparable trend in demand for named skills across sectors. The 2024 global report PDF, https://info.microsoft.com/rs/157-GQE-382/images/EN-CNTNT-Whitepaper-SRGCM13652-Work_Trend_Index_Annual_Report.pdf?version=0, returned HTTP 200 (2,393,767 bytes). No Malaysian microdata, CSV or open data licence was found.

### AWS and Access Partnership

The AWS / Access Partnership Malaysia infographic is dated March 2024. It reports a survey of 516 employers and 1,657 workers. Respondents include tech specialists, tech-adjacent workers and non-tech workers. It reports that 89% of employers prioritized AI hiring and 81% of those employers found hiring difficult. The infographic also projects AI task use to 2028; those are forecasts, not later observations. [21]

The PDF, https://accesspartnership.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Accelerating-AI-Skills_Malaysia-Infographics-Final.pdf, returned HTTP 200 (132,273 bytes). The global study page lists the Malaysia report as published in March 2024. An earlier 2022 AWS digital-skills study did not include Malaysia, so it does not add a Malaysian baseline. The infographic carries an AWS copyright notice; no open data licence or repeated Malaysia survey was found. [21]

### WEF Future of Jobs

The report series has editions in 2016, 2018, 2020, 2023 and 2025. Malaysia profiles are confirmed in 2020, 2023 and 2025. The 2020 profile combines country indicators with measures such as business-relevant skills and digital skills. The 2023 report includes Malaysia among 12 East Asia and Pacific economies. The 2025 profile reports 41% skill disruption for Malaysia, versus 39% globally, and lists skills of increasing use by 2030. [22–25]

These are employer outlook snapshots with different survey designs and broad skill groups. They do not provide a consistent time series for individual skills by Malaysian occupation. The 2025 report PDF URLs https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf and https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf returned HTTP 504 (936-byte gateway response). Requests for the 2020 and 2023 PDFs timed out after 30 seconds with curl exit 28. No public CSV was verified. [23–25]

WEF material is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 by default. It permits non-commercial sharing with attribution, but not distribution of modified material. Do not put WEF tables or charts into an Apache-licensed data file as if they were Apache data. Link to the report or cite short excerpts. [26]

### O*NET

The O*NET database archive contains quarterly and transitional releases. The tested points were 30.3 (May 2026) and 31.0 (August 2026). Software Skills first appeared in release 23.2; the occupation-specific In Demand flag was added in 27.1. The file’s latest content update is 30.3, even though database release 31.0 is newer. Its flags identify software examples frequently included in US employer postings, overall or for a specific occupation. Compare archived releases to inspect changes; the releases are snapshots, not a continuous posting-count feed. [27–28]

The 31.0 CSV ZIP, https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/database/db_31_0_csv.zip, returned HTTP 200 (16,237,378 bytes). The 30.3 CSV ZIP, https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/database/db_30_3_csv.zip, returned HTTP 200 (16,258,072 bytes). A sample Software Skills header is O*NET-SOC Code,Title,Workplace Example,Element ID,Element Name,Hot Technology,In Demand. First row: 11-1011.00,Chief Executives,Adobe Acrobat,2.E.5.b,Document management software,Y,N. [27–28]

Emerging Tasks first appeared in release 20.1 and was updated in 31.0. It contains new or revised tasks proposed for future collection, not observed hiring demand. Its 31.0 Excel file is a task feed, not a skill-demand history. [29]

The database is CC BY 4.0 with attribution and change notices. It is reusable in an Apache-licensed application when those notices remain with the O*NET-derived data. It remains US-only, and its posting-based skill file is software-focused. [30]

### Google Trends

Google has an official Trends API today, but it is still an alpha for a very limited number of testers. The documentation describes a rolling five-year window with daily, weekly, monthly and yearly intervals and geographic filters. The public UI supports manual CSV export and requests attribution to Google. [31–33]

A public Malaysia explore URL for accounting, https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=2021-01-01%202026-10-22&geo=MY&q=accounting, returned HTTP 429. No API sample was available without alpha access. Google’s Terms, effective 30 July 2026, prohibit automated access when it violates machine-readable instructions. Do not retry around a 429 or use hidden endpoints. [34]

Search interest is not job demand. Terms may be useful as a separate public-interest signal, but should not decide whether a Malaysian skill is rising or declining in hiring.

### MyMahir

MyMahir provides sector, role and skill labels, including basic, specific and technology skills. Its FAQ says the content mainly comes from the AI, Digital and Green Economy impact studies. The main 2024 study and later sector reports in 2025 are separate snapshots. The main study page has a View PDF button, but the page HTML did not expose a direct file URL; no CSV or public API was found. [35–36]

The MyMahir site terms forbid automated scraping and public or commercial reproduction, modification or distribution of its content without an express licence. They state “you may not reproduce, modify, display, sell, or distribute the Content” and bar automated tools used to “scrape,” “crawl,” or “spider” the site. No open data licence was found. A public Apache-licensed prototype should not copy the taxonomy or report data without written permission. [37]

### TalentCorp MyNSR

MyNSR links occupations to basic, specific and technology skills. Its FAQ says the pilot covers 68 occupations selected from MyCOL 2020/21 and 2022/23. Occupation pages add “Trending Skills” from job advertisements and mark “Emerging Skills,” but the cyber-security page says job-ad data is current only to 31 December 2021. The page displays a time-window label, not two dated numeric points for each skill. [38–39]

This is the closest Malaysian source to the engine’s role-to-skill layer. It does not provide a usable historical series, and no public CSV/API or open dataset licence was found. Treat its tags as a dated qualitative reference, not as a rising/declining verdict.

### TalentCorp MyCOL

The public MyCOL page lists editions from the 2015/16 pilot through 2024/25, with gaps between some editions. TalentCorp describes MyCOL as employer-developed and says it identifies occupations and skills that are in demand and hard to fill. This gives repeated occupation-level signals, but edition-to-edition skills are not published as a stable skill-by-role series. [40]

The 2024/25 PDF, https://www.talentcorp.com.my/images/uploads/publication/204/MyMahir-Malaysia-Critical-Occupations-List-MyCOL20242025-1773385490.pdf, returned HTTP 200 (21,685,713 bytes). Its cover identifies the 2024/25 edition; the first two pages are cover and contents pages. The site footer says All Rights Reserved. No open data licence was found. [41–42]

### Department of Skills Development NOSS

NOSS is a competency-standard catalogue organised by occupation and skill level. It does not report employer demand, vacancies or skill growth. The 2026 development notice is a plan for new or reviewed standards, not a labour-market time series. [43–44]

The 2026 development-list PDF, https://www.dsd.gov.my/images/03-perkhidmatan/noss/SENARAI_OF_NOSS_WIM_DAN_SOALAN_BAGI_PEMBANGUNAN_TAHUN_2026.pdf, returned HTTP 200 (109,659 bytes). Its title excerpt is “BAGI PEMBANGUNAN TAHUN 2026” (“for development in 2026”). The current registry link returned HTTP 200 but only a Google Drive viewer page, not the underlying registry file. No open data licence was displayed. [44]

## Unconfirmed

- No Malaysian edition of LinkedIn Skills on the Rise was found in 2025 or 2026. No open LinkedIn data export for the economic-graph series was confirmed.
- Coursera’s official Malaysia GSR profile values for 2021–2024 were not confirmed. The 2025 Malaysia learner profile and 2026 composite rank were confirmed.
- Stack Overflow’s 2026 Malaysia respondent count is not in the public top-ten country chart, and its raw 2026 results CSV returned HTTP 404. The archive is not evidence that Malaysia had zero respondents.
- WEF Malaysia profiles in the 2016 and 2018 editions were not confirmed. Malaysia profiles were confirmed for 2020, 2023 and 2025.
- No public MyMahir or MyNSR API, CSV export or open reuse licence was found. MyMahir’s direct study PDF URL was not exposed on the public page.
- The NOSS registry’s Google Drive viewer returned HTML instead of the registry data file. A direct data-file download was not confirmed.
- No broader, cross-industry Malaysian source with repeated dated demand values for individual skills was found. This is the review’s conclusion, not a claim that no such private dataset exists.
