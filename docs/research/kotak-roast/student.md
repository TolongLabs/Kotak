# Roast: Final-year Malaysian computer science student (target user)

## Verdict

- **Innovation**: 3/5 — Turning employer hiring rules into cited verdicts beats a generic CV chatbot, but a ledger that launches mostly empty and relies on fake sample cards will not win my trust during a real job hunt.
- **Live demo (easy and clear)**: 2/5 — Jumping between a student screen and a hiring manager screen to publish a labelled fake card makes the demo feel staged and hard to follow in a short video.
- **Feasibility as a pilot**: 2/5 — Five students who lose Friday to another hackathon cannot ship a two-sided app, a human review queue, an Economic Index pipeline, a RunPod extractor, and cat companions before 25 October at 7:59 AM MYT.

## Attacks

1.

- **Attack**: As a job-hunting CS senior facing a market where 74.3% of employed young graduates are underemployed (OpenDOSM Q2 2026 in `kotak.md`), I will close an app where key interview and portfolio verdicts `"start as Unknown, which is honest"` and the demo fills the gap with `"labelled sample employers"` under a `"Sample data"` banner.
- **Why it matters**: This hits Innovation & Creativity (30%) and Problem-Solution Fit (25%) because a citation engine with no real Malaysian answers on launch feels like an empty shell.
- **Question the team must answer**: Why would a stressed final-year student trust or return to Kotak today when most questions in the box have no real Malaysian employer evidence behind them?
- **What would fix it**: Seed the launch database before Sunday with at least 10 real, cited Malaysian tech job ads and engineering hiring pages from companies like Grab, Setel, Carsome, and MoneyLion so every verdict starts with real local data.

2.

- **Attack**: The demo script asks judges to `"Switch to the hiring lead's view and publish a sample company's card"` just to move one verdict from `"Unknown"` to `"Split"`, which makes the trend layer look like a manual toggle button.
- **Why it matters**: This hurts Clarity of Proposal (20%) and Feasibility of Build (25%) because role-playing two users mid-demo confuses cold viewers and hides whether the AI signal layer works on its own.
- **Question the team must answer**: How can you prove your app tracks live AI trends when a human has to pretend to be a hiring manager and type in a fake card during the demo?
- **What would fix it**: Keep the live demo entirely inside the student view and trigger the `"What changed"` update by running a real public employer page through the RunPod extraction pipeline on screen.

3.

- **Attack**: Having `"Live2D cats guide each step, VTuber-style"` alongside an `"Akinator-style intake"`, an employer portal, and a review screen feels gimmicky to anxious job hunters and cannot be built by 25 October at 7:59 AM MYT when `"Friday stays light"` for `sdp-hack`.
- **Why it matters**: This threatens Feasibility of Build (25%) because splitting roughly two build days across mascot animation, quiz trees, and two portals will leave the core role-matching loop unfinished.
- **Question the team must answer**: Which single workflow will actually run without bugs by Sunday at 7:59 AM MYT after `sdp-hack` eats Friday?
- **What would fix it**: Cut the Live2D cat companion, the Akinator quiz, and the employer publishing flow tonight so the team only builds the four-step student flow and the evidence pipeline.

4.

- **Attack**: Making the hiring lead `"70% of the pitch"` by expecting managers to publicly `"declare the junior hiring bar in two minutes"` ignores that employers keep test rules secret so candidates cannot game their interviews.
- **Why it matters**: This hits Problem-Solution Fit (25%) and Innovation & Creativity (30%) because if employers refuse to publish cards, your main Malaysian data source stays empty.
- **Question the team must answer**: Why would a busy Malaysian engineering manager publicly reveal their interview stages and AI rules on an unknown student site when assessment cheating is rising?
- **What would fix it**: Replace self-reported employer cards with automated RunPod extraction from public Malaysian job descriptions, company engineering blogs, and university placement surveys.

5.

- **Attack**: Locking `"The box"` to six broad questions like `"Do employers want AI skills from juniors? Settled at launch by three surveys"` gives me obvious trivia instead of showing what specific Malaysian employers—from Cyberjaya software houses to GLCs and MNCs—test in technical rounds.
- **Why it matters**: This weakens Problem-Solution Fit (25%) because CS seniors already know employers like AI and need role-specific skill bars and salary floors to avoid underemployment.
- **Question the team must answer**: How does knowing that three macro surveys value AI skills change what I study this weekend for a junior backend role versus an AI engineer role in Kuala Lumpur?
- **What would fix it**: Replace the six generic industry questions with role-specific requirement breakdowns that show which concrete tasks, stacks, and pay bands appear in Malaysian MNC, GLC, and startup hiring.

6.

- **Attack**: Scoring my role fit when `"the demo uses the closest O*NET occupation"` for missing roles like AI engineer and drives outlooks from a feed where `"The Economic Index measures Claude usage, not jobs"` makes the `"score out of 100"` hard to trust.
- **Why it matters**: This hits Innovation & Creativity (30%) and Feasibility of Build (25%) because judges will see that US occupation checklists and global Claude chat volume do not measure junior job openings in Malaysia.
- **Question the team must answer**: Why should a Malaysian CS graduate bet their final semester on US O\*NET skill lists and Claude prompt counts instead of local employer demand?
- **What would fix it**: Ground role skill lists directly in TalentCorp's MyMahir and MDEC Malaysia Digital frameworks, and combine AI usage data with DOSM and MyCOL shortage figures.

7.

- **Attack**: Having `"Every role and step tagged to target 4.4 (skills for jobs) or 8.5 (decent work for young people)"` turns SDG mapping into a rubber stamp because every job-hunting step on earth fits those two targets.
- **Why it matters**: This hurts Innovation & Creativity (30%) and Problem-Solution Fit (25%) because the rubric asks for SDG links that `"change a user's choices"` and are `"meaningful rather than decorative"`.
- **Question the team must answer**: How does putting SDG 4.4 or 8.5 on every single study step help me choose between building AI for clean energy (SDG 7), healthcare (SDG 3), or decent work (SDG 8)?
- **What would fix it**: Let students pick an SDG industry track in Step 4 so the reskilling plan recommends portfolio projects and hackathons tied to specific sustainability problems across all 17 SDGs.

## What to keep

- **Rules decide while AI only extracts and explains**: Keeping `"Rules decide; the AI reads and explains"` directly answers the organisers' warning against chatbot wrappers and stops the app from inventing jobs, courses, or statistics.
- **Two explicit futures on Split verdicts**: Showing two concrete paths when evidence is `"Split"`—such as `"if algorithm tests stay"` versus `"if AI-assisted tests spread"`—gives students a practical way to hedge their study time.
- **Proof-building steps in the study plan**: Mixing free courses with `"proof-building steps such as shipping a project or entering a hackathon"` matches how CS graduates actually prove their skills to engineering managers.
