# Malaysian job demand sources with history

This report checks which published Malaysian job-demand sources provide dated history at useful levels and have tested, reusable access for a public prototype.

## Summary

- DOSM Employment Statistics is the longest checked quarterly series: Q1 2015–Q2 2026, with vacancies by sector and broad skill category. It does not break demand down by occupation.
- DOSM Job Market Insights adds sector, major occupation group and selected roles from Q3 2019–Q2 2026. Its reports are downloadable, but no open reuse licence was found and the source warns the ads are not the full vacancy population.
- MDEC publishes useful digital-job signals, but its 2024 snapshot is marked confidential. Its 2021 public article gives only two aggregate points. Neither is an open history feed.
- Techmap exposes monthly Malaysia totals from January 2020 to September 2026. The number of contributing sources changes sharply. Detailed records require a paid feed.
- SEEK, LinkedIn, MEOS and recruiter guides do not provide a recurring, open Malaysia job-ad series. LinkedIn has Malaysia coverage in 2021 and 2022; MEOS sector figures are regional, not Malaysia-specific.
- The best open posting files are snapshots: a Kaggle JobStreet dataset with dated rows, and Lynceus with confirmed Malaysian current listings. Neither preserves a full Malaysia history. No numerical values below were guessed.

## Details

### DOSM Employment Statistics

The Quarterly Employment Survey measures formal private-sector jobs, vacancies and jobs created by economic activity and skill category. The Q4 2017 release says its historical table covers Q1 2015–Q4 2017. The current series continues to Q2 2026. [1][2]

The Q2 2026 report gives 195.7 thousand vacancies. It breaks out five sectors and skilled, semi-skilled and low-skilled categories. The public report is directly downloadable, but I found no open licence for copying the tables into an Apache-licensed app. It does not identify occupations or named skills. [1]

### DOSM Big Data Analytics: Job Market Insights

The online-posting series starts at Q3 2019. DOSM’s Q3 2023 chart gives 74,018 postings for Q3 2019 and 338,305 for Q3 2023. The latest Q2 2026 report gives 555,509 postings. The release series is quarterly. [3][4]

The reports include economic activities, MASCO occupation groups and selected roles. The Q2 2026 sample lists 19,899 Software Developer postings. The report describes its source as online offerings on private recruitment platforms; a 2021 release warns that web-scraped postings do not represent the full vacancy population. No historical skill-demand series or open reuse licence was found. [3]

### MDEC digital vacancy reports

MDEC’s public May 2021 article says its five-platform digital vacancy count rose from about 19,000 in June 2020 to over 56,000 in April 2021. It lists broad role areas and skills, but publishes only those two aggregate dates. [5]

MDEC’s Q3 2024 Digital Talent Snapshot reports a separate quarterly series for Q1–Q3 2024 and adds job-title and skill signals. Its first page says the slides are strictly confidential and may not be circulated or reproduced without written consent. I therefore report its dates and restrictions, not its underlying counts. The 2021 article does not provide an open data licence either. [6]

### Techmap Malaysia

The public Data Explorer says Malaysia monthly postings are available since January 2020. Its table shows 6,191 in January 2020 and 48,752 in September 2026. The source count changed from two to 36 across those endpoints, and reached 50 in July 2026. Treat changes as coverage-sensitive. [7]

The public table is national only. Techmap’s product description lists titles, descriptions, locations and skills in the detailed feed. The Malaysia bulk feed is paid; the AWS listing limits the purchased feed to individual use and prohibits resale. I found no free Malaysia sample or open-data licence. [7]

### JobStreet by SEEK

The public report archive contains salary, hiring and workplace studies from 2021 through 2026, but I found no published Malaysian job-ad volume index or open aggregate feed. SEEK’s terms require an application and approval before credentials are issued. The reviewed developer docs describe approved, hirer-linked recruitment-software integrations; no public demand-data endpoint surfaced in those docs. [8]

The existing source notes already record the candidate-site robots and scraping restrictions. This addition confirms the partner API terms and the absence of a public demand series.

### LinkedIn Jobs on the Rise

I found Malaysian coverage in 2021 and 2022. The 2021 Southeast Asia article links a shared list for Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines and Thailand and says the demand signal covers April–October 2020. It does not explain a Malaysia-only ranking formula or publish counts. LinkedIn’s 2021 global article describes a change from its five-year Emerging Jobs Report to a list of 15 fastest-growing trends from 2020. [9]

The 2022 article includes Malaysia among four countries. It ranks titles by growth from January 2017 through July 2021, requiring sustained growth across LinkedIn’s membership and a meaningful 2021 base. The Malaysia titles include GIS Analyst, Medical Laboratory Scientist, IT Associate, Fraud Analyst and Data Engineer. [10]

The 2023 Southeast Asia list covers Singapore, Indonesia and the Philippines, not Malaysia. I found no Malaysia edition for 2024–2026. These are annual member-data snapshots, not an occupation-level series of job ads. LinkedIn’s User Agreement bars scraping or copying its services without consent. [10][11]

### ManpowerGroup Employment Outlook Survey

The NEO is the share of employers expecting hiring to rise minus the share expecting it to fall for the coming quarter. ManpowerGroup says the survey has run since 1962. That is the global survey history, not a Malaysia history. [12]

The Malaysia-hosted pages report APME regional figures by quarter. The oldest sector detail I located is Q4 2024: APME NEO +27%, with Financials & Real Estate at +37% and IT at +35%. Q4 2026 is the latest regional point checked: NEO +33%, with Information at +42%, Tech & IT Services at +41%, and Finance & Insurance at +40%. The Malaysia article says there is no Malaysia-specific Q4 2026 NEO. These regional figures must not be presented as Malaysian forecasts. [12]

### Hays

Hays published Malaysia employer survey results in 2025 and 2026. The 2025 survey gathered 3,670 hiring managers across six locations in late 2024. The 2026 release discusses mid-level hiring difficulty and transferable skills. These are annual survey snapshots and intentions, not counted postings. [13]

The 2026 page names learning and upskilling, communication and adapting to change as sought-after skills. The 2025 and 2026 survey percentages use different report cycles and should not be treated as a consistent vacancy series. Full salary guides require a form; I found no open reuse licence. [13]

### Robert Walters

Robert Walters publishes annual Malaysia salary surveys and hiring guides. I located 2024 and 2025 country guides and the 2026 salary survey page. The pages describe placements, salaries and year-ahead hiring views; they do not publish a repeated occupation-level vacancy count. [14]

The free report is offered through a contact form. No public data export, API or open reuse licence was found. Treat roles and skills in these guides as qualitative annual signals. [14]

### Randstad

Randstad’s Malaysia guides cover 2024, 2025 and 2026. The 2026 page says the guide contains salary benchmarks for more than 500 roles across nine sectors and includes a survey of 982 respondents. The 2025 guide reports hiring intentions, not observed vacancy counts. [15]

The PDFs are downloadable, but the reports are copyrighted and no open data licence was found. Role and sector salary tables do not create a consistent demand time series. [15]

### Michael Page

Michael Page’s 2024, 2025 and 2026 Talent Trends reports contain employer and professional survey findings. Its 2026 salary material lists roles and sectors, but the Salary Benchmark Tool uses placements and advertised roles from the past 12 months. That is a moving snapshot, not a dated history of job volume. [16]

The reports and salary guide pages provide qualitative role and skill signals. No reusable raw vacancy feed or open licence was found. [16]

### PIKOM Economic and Digital Job Market Outlook

PIKOM’s 2025 report was published in December 2025 with JobStreet and other data providers. Its annual tables show advertised salary benchmarks by job level and industry from 2013 or 2015 through 2025, depending on table. That is salary history, not job-demand volume. [17]

The PDF downloaded successfully, but it says all rights are reserved and reproduction needs prior written permission. It is not suitable as a public data feed for this prototype. [17]

### Indeed Hiring Lab

The public Indeed Hiring Lab portal lists country and sector data for Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States. Malaysia is not listed. Its public Job Postings Index uses a seven-day moving average and a 1 February 2020 base. [18]

The Hiring Lab API supports national, regional and sector datasets with daily or monthly updates, but Indeed issues API keys to partners and researchers. An unauthenticated GraphQL request returned HTTP 401. The API’s Malaysia coverage remains unknown. Malaysia-site terms require written permission for automated access outside the robots.txt rules; robots.txt disallows Malaysia job paths. [18]

### Hiredly

Hiredly’s Market Insights area has reports and guides, but I found no published Malaysian vacancy-count history or public data API. Its robots.txt has Allow: / entries for named crawlers and no CCBot entry. The terms page loaded, but its automated-access clause could not be confirmed from extracted text. Do not infer permission from the robots file alone. [19]

### Maukerja

I found no published job-count report or official public API. Its robots.txt disallows job, search and API paths. Its terms prohibit scraping, crawling or systematic extraction without prior written consent. [20]

### Ricebowl

I found no published job-count report or official public API. Its robots.txt disallows job, search and API paths. Its terms prohibit scraping, crawling or systematic extraction without prior written consent. Ricebowl and Maukerja identify the same platform operator in their terms. [21]

### MYFutureJobs / SOCSO

MYFutureJobs’ Labour Market Exchange describes reports, wage guides and occupation classifications, and links occupations to current vacancies. I found no public dated vacancy file or official API. The candidate-search subdomain’s robots URL returned application HTML, so its robots policy was not confirmed. [22]

The terms PDF limits content use to personal, non-commercial purposes and restricts copying, distribution and network storage without SOCSO permission. The site root robots file only showed WordPress API restrictions; it does not establish the candidate subdomain’s rules. [22]

### eMASCO / Ministry of Human Resources

The eMASCO page shows employment statistics as of March 2026 by MASCO major group. The Malay heading is “Statistik Pekerjaan setakat bulan Mac 2026” (“Employment statistics as of March 2026”). It is one snapshot, not a series. I found no public data export, API or open reuse licence. [23]

### Kaggle JobStreet Job Postings

Kaggle’s dataset API returned HTTP 200 for the 52.7 MB ZIP. The CSV has 69,024 records and 166.5 MB when uncompressed. The listingDate range is 23 March 2023 through 8 May 2025. The sample first row is Procurement Executive (Contract), Negeri Sembilan, posted 21 March 2024. [24]

Kaggle labels the dataset Apache 2.0. Its description says it is updated weekly from a scraping script, but the downloaded version has no archived weekly snapshots; requesting version 1 returned HTTP 404. The Kaggle licence does not confirm permission from JobStreet, whose existing terms prohibit scraping without consent. Treat reuse of the scraped data as unconfirmed. [24]

### Hugging Face JobStreet mirror

The Hugging Face card describes another JobStreet-derived dataset. It lists 59,306 rows, 123 MB, a CC BY 4.0 label and weekly scraping updates. I could not confirm its full posting-date range or a historical version archive. Its viewer search API timed out after 15 seconds. The claimed dataset licence does not establish upstream JobStreet permission. [25]

### Lynceus Open Job Index

The Hugging Face dataset contains current roles from employer career pages and ATS feeds. Its card shows 234,605 roles across 6,072 companies and global posted dates from 14 May 2015 to 5 October 2026. A search API sample returned Malaysian job titles with posted dates in August and September 2026. [26]

The sampled records show a Malaysia footprint, but they do not establish the Malaysia-specific date range or full source coverage. The dataset contains title, company, location, remote status and posted date; it does not provide a historical snapshot archive, sector labels or skills. It is CC BY 4.0 as listed on the card. [26]

### Scholarweave OpenJobData v2

The card describes current listings, daily changes and detected closures. It labels the dataset Apache 2.0 and shows first_seen_at dates from 24 May to 15 October 2026. The posted_at column contains implausible years from 0026 to 2030, so use first_seen_at only after validation. [27]

The tested rows API returned a United States role. A Malaysia search request timed out after 20 seconds. Malaysian coverage and the Malaysia-specific time range remain unconfirmed. [27]

### World Bank / Burning Glass Malaysia study

The World Bank paper analyses more than 600,000 unique Malaysian online job postings from May 2016 to December 2018. It maps titles to MASCO 2013 and reports 8,221 skills grouped into 633 clusters and 28 families. This is a detailed historical role-skill study, not a live feed. [28]

The report PDF is public, but the raw Burning Glass posting data are proprietary and were not offered for download. No open data licence for the underlying records was found. [28]

### Headcount Labs API

Headcount Labs documents an API with posting and change endpoints, first-seen and last-seen dates, and Malaysia coverage. Its public sample JSON downloaded successfully. The Malaysia count endpoint returned HTTP 401 without a bearer API key. The history start date and the amount of retained Malaysian history are not stated. [29]

The provider’s CC BY 4.0 licence covers its own selection, arrangement, identifiers and derived fields. It excludes the employers’ and job boards’ posting text, names, logos and personal data. This could support a prototype only after the team receives an API key and limits reuse to fields the licence covers. [29]

### Common Crawl

The CC-MAIN-2026-43 index covers a crawl from 5–18 October 2026. Three JobStreet homepage queries produced one 404 response saying there were no captures and two 503 responses. Hiredly queries returned 503; Maukerja queries returned 503/504; Ricebowl queries returned 503. I confirmed no positive job-board listing capture. [30]

Hiredly’s robots file has no CCBot-specific entry; JobStreet, Indeed, Maukerja and Ricebowl restrict job paths in their robots files. Common Crawl’s terms also say captured pages may remain subject to the source owner’s terms. The index is not a reliable or reusable Malaysia demand feed. [19][20][21][30]

## Unconfirmed

- LinkedIn: no Malaysia edition was found before 2021 or for 2024–2026. Search absence is not proof that no edition exists. [9][10]
- ManpowerGroup: the global start date is 1962, but I could not confirm the first Malaysia-specific NEO observation or a Malaysian sector-by-quarter archive. The latest checked Malaysia post explicitly says Q4 2026 has no Malaysia-specific NEO. [12]
- Hiredly: its terms page did not expose an automated-access clause in the text extraction. The site’s robots file does not name CCBot. [19]
- Hugging Face OpenJobData: the country field exists, but the tested Malaysia search timed out. Do not assume the dataset contains usable Malaysian rows. [27]
- Zenodo, Mendeley Data and university repositories: I did not verify a Malaysia job-posting dataset with source provenance, dates, licence and a working sample. Zenodo’s broad search returned unrelated records; this does not establish that no dataset exists. [31]
- Kaggle JobStreet dataset: Apache 2.0 is the licence displayed by the uploader. Permission to republish the scraped source listings was not confirmed. [24]
- Headcount Labs: Malaysia coverage is visible, but API history depth cannot be checked without an API key. [29]
