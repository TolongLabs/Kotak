## Findings

### 1. Event lineage

- sdchack.my calls SDC "the first dedicated hackathon circuit at Xiamen University Malaysia". The homepage names no past editions, past winners or judges. It credits XMUM AI Club and XMUM Rotaract Club as organisers and lists Aiversecity under "Powered by". ([sdchack.my](https://sdchack.my/))
- Searches of XMUM news, Devpost and the web found no earlier SDC, XMUM AI Club or XMUM-hosted hackathon. The XMUM news pages that turned up show students competing at events run by others. For example, Team ViXual won "Second Runner-Up and Best Innovation Awards after an intensive 48-hour hackathon" at IPCC 2025 at UTP, 22–24 Aug. ([XMUM news][xmum-ipcc])
- The club's Instagram account is "XMUM Artificial Intelligence Club (MINDS)", with 1,012 followers and 180 posts. The SDC account has 828 followers and 69 posts. Posts can't be read without logging in. ([@xmum_ai_club](https://www.instagram.com/xmum_ai_club/), [@sdc_xmumhack](https://www.instagram.com/sdc_xmumhack/))

[xmum-ipcc]: https://www.xmu.edu.my/index.php/school-energy-and-chemical-engineering/posts/news/xmum-team-wins-awards-international-process

### 2. Aiversecity, the Domain 1 sponsor

- The site's mission statement: "Our mission is to help people, businesses, and societies design AI that serves humanity — not replaces it." It also says "we build not just AI applications, but AI ecosystems that think with us, learn with us, and evolve with us", and its tagline is "Where Humanity Meets Its Next Evolution". The site names no products, founders or customers. It is a single page built with Lovable (its og:image is hosted on lovable.dev). ([aiversecity.com](https://aiversecity.com/))
- The terms say "Services include websites, platforms, courses, AI tools, and APIs". The privacy policy lists "course recommendations, analytics, progress tracking" and covers "users, learners, partners, job applicants". Enterprise contracts can include SOC2 or ISO27001 attestations. The governing-law clause reads "Laws of Malaysia (or specify jurisdiction)", with arbitration in Kuala Lumpur under AIAC rules, and the terms say "You must not use outputs in life-critical contexts without human oversight". Effective date: 21 October 2025. Both pages are drafts: the terms say "This Terms draft … finalize with counsel and consider local consumer laws", and the privacy policy says "Finalize and localize with qualified privacy counsel". Treat them as weak evidence. ([terms](https://aiversecity.com/terms), [privacy](https://aiversecity.com/privacy))
- linkedin.com/company/aiversecity returns 404. Searches for "Aiversecity", "AIverseCity" and "ORION AI Youth" found no other official profile. ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/aiversecity))
- No Aiversecity page names a legal entity (Sdn Bhd, Berhad or PLT) or an SSM number. The footer says only "© 2025 AIVERSECITY. All rights reserved.", and the domain was registered on 15 June 2025 ([RDAP][aiv-rdap]). SSM's e-Info search needs a paid login, so it wasn't checked.
- The CEO's LinkedIn profile names **Gordan Vong** as founder and CEO. It lists "Founder & Chief Executive Officer - AIVERSECITY (Current) Jan 2026 - Present" in Kuala Lumpur, and describes the company as "a global AI learning ecosystem designed to transform people from AI users into AI architects". The profile was read from Exa's index (crawled 15 September 2026), because the live page needs a login. It names no customers or partners. Its headline currently starts "CEO of VHYSER", and its January 2026 start date is later than the site's own dates (domain June 2025, policies effective 21 October 2025) ([profile][gordan-vong]).
- At the briefing the emcee called Aiversecity "a human-centred AI platform that makes AI more accessible, responsible and impactful". Christine Cheng spoke for it "on behalf of AIverseCity and CEO Gordon Wong" (the transcript marks the name "as heard"; his own profile spells it Gordan Vong). She closed her remarks with "put people first". ([briefing transcript](/docs/sources/opening-briefing/transcript.md#messages), 00:15:53–00:18:45)
- Aiversecity runs ORION AI Youth Leaders. Registrants are enrolled automatically unless they decline (handbook §13.1–13.2). Handbook §13.3 says members "may receive" benefits "at Aiversecity's discretion", which "may include" "career and internship matching with companies in Aiversecity's business network", and that the Organiser doesn't guarantee them. §13.6 lists an Employer Showcase and investor pitching. ([participant handbook §13](/docs/sources/drive.google.com/participant-handbook.md#13-aiversecity-orion-ai-youth-leaders-sponsor-community), [§13.6](/docs/sources/drive.google.com/participant-handbook.md#136-orion-ai-youth-community-membership-benefits))
- The brief carries the line "AI should be a partner to people, not a replacement for them". It defines success as: "the output visibly changes when the trend data changes, and the SDG link is meaningful rather than decorative". ([Domain 1 brief, background](/docs/sources/drive.google.com/domain-1-aiversecity.md#1-background), [what success looks like](/docs/sources/drive.google.com/domain-1-aiversecity.md#7-what-success-looks-like))
- Mazen at the briefing: "For Domain 1, what you must target is a mix of businesses and government." ([transcript 01:21:52](/docs/sources/opening-briefing/transcript.md#messages))

### 4. Malaysian precedent from 2023 to 2026

- **Talentbank Tech Hackathon, Cohort 1** is the closest precedent found. The theme was "Build Asia's Career OS with AI", and the grand finale was 22 Aug 2026. Champion (RM 10,000): GongBaiWan. 1st runner-up: Notts-Hackers. 2nd runner-up: J2CH. The page lists team members and mentors but not what each team built. The Cohort 2 modules include "Career Path Navigator", "AI Career Coach", "Fair Pay Engine" and "Living Portfolio". The build standard is "Production-ready only". The Adoption Pool has two tiers and is "Awarded only for exceptional work", so not all submitted code is adopted. ([techhackathon.com](https://www.techhackathon.com/))
- **Great Malaysia AI Hackathon 2025** was run by APU with AWS and MDEC. It drew 1,741 participants and had a RM110,000 prize pool. Champion: "Ctrl + Chill" (APU) with MySelamat, an AI flood-rescue system that combines crowdsourced social data with route planning. Runners-up: Solo Panda (UM) and Kiwi Birds (Heriot-Watt). ([APU news][apu-ctrl-chill])
- No Malaysian winner in reskilling, careers or education was verified at a primary source apart from Talentbank. Notable winners elsewhere address floods, smart cities, donations and insurance (see [Open questions](#open-questions)).

[apu-ctrl-chill]: https://www.apu.edu.my/news/apus-ctrl-chill-team-crowned-champion-great-malaysia-ai-hackathon-2025

### 5. Malaysian government and industry context

- **Hays.** "56 per cent of working professionals in Malaysia use AI tools at work", but "only 26 per cent have received training to use such tools". The source is the 2025 Hays Asia Salary Guide, in a press release dated 7 Mar 2025. The same release reports Malaysian software-development demand down 32% (2022–2024) and infrastructure roles down 46% since 2022. ([Hays Malaysia][hays-future-of-work])
- **The 600,000 figure.** It originates with HR Minister Steven Sim (10 Oct 2024): "Approximately 600,000 workers have been identified as needing reskilling over the next three to five years." It came from phase 1 of a study by the HR Ministry, TalentCorp and industry covering 10 sectors. MOSTI repeated it in Jan 2025; Malay Mail's page returned 403, so that version was checked only via search snippets. ([Bernama][bernama-2024])
- **The figure is now 697,000.** HR Minister R. Ramanan, 24 Jun 2026: "a TalentCorp study found approximately 697,000 jobs are at risk of being affected by technological advancements and the green economy within the next three to five years if workers do not upskill themselves". Bernama also reports, in its own words, that "He said AI is not a threat to employment at present". Read the 697,000 as jobs affected, not lost, and conditional on workers not upskilling. ([Bernama][bernama-2026])
  - The figure isn't in the Hansard record of that day's oral answer, Question 9 ([Hansard, 24 June 2026][hansard-2026-06-24]), so it may come from a written reply. TalentCorp's own release of 17 January 2026 said "around 685,000 Malaysian workers could be significantly impacted" ([TalentCorp release][tc-2026-01-17]).
  - Press coverage attributes the 697,000 and "around 120 new job roles" to TalentCorp's Impact Study across 22 sectors, phases 1 and 2, against the 600,000's 10 sectors ([Malay Mail, 1 May 2026][mm-2026-05-01]). No TalentCorp or ministry document checked contains either figure, so cite both as press-reported.
- **TalentCorp Impact Study, phase 1.** About 620,000 employees: "Approximately 18% of the workforce within the analysed sectors is projected to be significantly affected". The study covers 10 sectors making up ~60% of GDP and finds "around 60 emerging job roles". It offers "career pathways and pivoting strategies for 84 highly impacted roles, alongside 864 basic and role-specific skills". "Phase 2 … is currently in progress." ([TalentCorp](https://www.talentcorp.com.my/impact-study/))
- **MyCOL.** The latest edition listed is 2024/2025, published as PDFs. TalentCorp describes it as covering the "skills imbalance across 18 economic sectors", led by TalentCorp under KESUMA. The 2024/2025 PDF refers to "the 66 occupations in the National list", and a separate Regional List exists. ([TalentCorp MyCOL](https://www.talentcorp.com.my/mycol/))
- **MyMahir** ("by TalentCorp Malaysia") lists In-Demand and Emerging roles, "800+ skills", "140++ Available Training", a Roles & Skills Explorer and an AI Readiness Index. No public API was seen. ([mymahir.my](https://www.mymahir.my/))
- **HRD Corp.** Employers can claim up to 100% of training costs for HRD Corp-approved courses. The rules sit in the Allowable Cost Matrix, and e-LATiH offers free courses; its course counts conflict between pages (see [prior art](/docs/research/prior-art-sdg-courses.md)). ([hrdcorp.gov.my](https://hrdcorp.gov.my/), [e-LATiH](https://elatih.hrdcorp.gov.my))
- **MYFutureJobs** (PERKESO) offers "AI-driven skill-based job matching", MYMidCareer40 and Labour Market Exchange supply-and-demand statistics. Career transition programmes were not seen on the cited page. ([myfuturejobs.gov.my](https://myfuturejobs.gov.my/))
- **ILMIA** hosts the KILM labour indicators and a data mart. Downloads require registration, the visible data dates from 2016–2017, and some pages show injected spam. ([ILMIA][ilmia-datamart])

## Implications for our Domain 1 entry

- **Use the current figures.** Cite Hays 56%/26% and the press-reported 697,000 (Jun 2026) with the caveat Bernama reports in the same article that "AI is not a threat to employment at present". Present 600,000 as the Oct 2024 baseline, and say that MOSTI repeated a figure the HR Ministry and TalentCorp produced. Getting this right is a quick Clarity and credibility win ([§5](#5-malaysian-government-and-industry-context)).
- **Build two views: HR or agency, and learner.** Organisers asked for businesses plus government. Aiversecity's terms (a draft, so weak evidence) mention enterprise contracts, and ORION says it may offer employer matching. A workforce heatmap for an HR manager or a TalentCorp-style planner, alongside the individual view, fits all three ([§2](#2-aiversecity-the-domain-1-sponsor), [§5](#5-malaysian-government-and-industry-context)).
- **Ground role and skill lists in Malaysian public data.** MyMahir emerging roles, the TalentCorp Impact Study's 60 emerging roles and 864 basic and role-specific skills, and MyCOL 2024/2025 should anchor the taxonomy, so every role cites a Malaysian source. Pathways should link HRD Corp-claimable courses or e-LATiH free courses ([§5](#5-malaysian-government-and-industry-context)).
- **Show the AI as advisory.** Aiversecity's own draft terms require "human oversight" in life-critical contexts and call outputs "probabilistic". A visible "estimate, you decide" design, plus two scenarios, matches the sponsor's legal and brand voice ([§2](#2-aiversecity-the-domain-1-sponsor)).
- **Expect "can you adopt this?" questions.** With no judges named, assume sponsor and industry judges. Talentbank's "Production-ready only" bar and its two-tier Adoption Pool, "Awarded only for exceptional work", show that a Malaysian career hackathon looks for code its sponsors can adopt, though only for the strongest entries. A clean, documented repo and an honest data-lineage panel help ([§3](#3-judges-and-mentors), [§4](#4-malaysian-precedent-from-2023-to-2026)).
- **Use the mentor slot for the business case.** The KrackedDevs mentors (technical and business) or Ravenna Chen (business strategy) fit best. Their 21–23 Oct windows fall before the 25 Oct deadline ([§3](#3-judges-and-mentors)).

## Open questions

Items marked "checked in issue 20" were searched again in issue 20 without finding a primary source.

- Whether any XMUM AI Club event before SDC existed. Instagram posts need a login, and no news page or Devpost page was found.
- Aiversecity's legal entity, products and customers. Checked in issue 20; no primary source names them.
- What the Talentbank winners built, and which universities they come from. The live winners block lists only names and mentors. A search snippet attributes GongBaiWan to Heriot-Watt Malaysia and Notts-Hackers to Nottingham Malaysia.
- A primary source for the 697,000 and "120 new roles" figures. Checked in issue 20 against Hansard and 37 TalentCorp releases; neither figure appears, so both stay press-reported until TalentCorp publishes its phase 2 summary.
- MyCOL 2025/2026: an RFP was issued in Feb 2025, but no published edition was found.
- Champions, projects and dates for UMHackathon 2025 (only the 1st runner-up, AmanahBlock, was found), CodeNection 2025, Kracked Devs events, and Malaysian GDSC Solution Challenge Top-100 teams (2023 "learnit", 2024 Moodee, WaterGuard, WhyFlood). The Google winners page redirected and could not be read.
- V HACK 2025 (USM) results: champion HydroNet (UM), smart manholes. The USM page returned 404, so this rests on search snippets only.
