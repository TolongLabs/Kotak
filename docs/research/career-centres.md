# Malaysian university career centres: services, tools and AI-skills software

This report answers what public-university career centres do, what platforms serve them, what vendors offer, and how a student team could pilot a tool.

## Summary

- Career centres combine career guidance with employability programmes, employer links, job and internship access, and graduate-outcome work. Six public universities show this pattern. [1–11]
- National tools already include TalentCorp MyNext, PERKESO MYFutureJobs, MOHE GREaT/SKPG, and MOHE MySISWA@JoC. Official pages confirm campus use at UM, UPM, and USM. [12–23]
- Universities also use campus-run tools such as UKM MyKarier and Job on Campus, UTM Job Match and Job on Campus, and UiTM JobShop. [24–28]
- Career-services platforms manage appointments, events, employers, placements, and outcomes. Lightcast is the clearest product reviewed with skill-demand forecasts; its AI material describes demand for AI skills, not AI-caused job losses. [29–40]
- UTMXCITE offers a student innovation route. UKM’s student-built orientation app is a campus adoption precedent, but no career-centre-specific student pilot programme was confirmed. [41–43]
- A live-data pilot needs university approval on PDPA scope and data roles. The 2024 amendments added controller, processor, DPO, breach-notification, and portability provisions with staged commencement. [44–49]

### 4. Student pilot routes and personal data

UTMXCITE is UTM’s student innovation and technology-entrepreneurship centre. A team could seek a UTMCC sponsor and use UTMXCITE for prototype support; its public page does not describe a career-centre pilot scheme. [41]

UKM provides an adoption precedent. It launched MESRA@UKM, an orientation app designed by UKM students. A UKM journal article later evaluated its use during the 2018 orientation week. This is a campus-service precedent, not a career-tool deployment. [42, 43]

Recommended pilot sequence, inferred from these precedents:

1. Ask a career-centre director to sponsor one student-facing use case and define what success means.
2. Show the centre a working prototype with synthetic data. Do not use identifiable student records in the demo.
3. Before a live trial, get written approval from the university’s privacy or legal office. Agree which party is controller and which is processor. [44, 47]
4. Keep a live pilot opt-in and narrow. Give students a clear notice, get consent where required, collect only needed data, restrict access, set a deletion date, and let an adviser review recommendations. [44–46]

The PDPA 2010 regulates personal-data processing in commercial transactions. Its seven principles cover general, notice and choice, disclosure, security, retention, data integrity, and access. Where the Act applies, data subjects have rights to be informed about processing and to access or correct their data. The Federal and State Governments are exempt, but the sources do not resolve whether the exemption applies to a particular public university or student-team/vendor arrangement. [44–46]

The 2024 amendments renamed data users as data controllers, extended the Security Principle to processors, added DPO and breach-notification duties, and created a data-portability right. Commencement was staged: 1 January, 1 April, and 1 June 2025. A controller must notify the Commissioner when it believes a breach occurred; it must notify affected people if significant harm is caused or likely. A DPO is required if processing involves more than 20,000 data subjects, more than 10,000 sensitive-data subjects, or regular and systematic monitoring. [47–49]

## Unconfirmed

- No official vendor or university pages reviewed confirmed Symplicity, Handshake, 12twenty, or Lightcast as a customer of a named Malaysian university. This does not show they are unused.
- APU is a confirmed HubbedIn customer, but it is private. Talentbank lists public-university partners; actual Career Passport deployment and paid-customer status at each partner remain unconfirmed.
- No career-centre-specific student pilot programme was confirmed. UTMXCITE is broader innovation support, and MESRA@UKM was an orientation tool.
- UTM’s own Job on Campus scheme is confirmed. Use of the separate national MySISWA@JoC platform at UTM was not confirmed.
- A university legal or privacy office must decide how the government exemption and “commercial transactions” scope apply to a specific public-university pilot and student team. [44, 46]
