# Hiring-bar evidence

Which public statistics show what Malaysian employers now want from AI-native junior tech candidates, to test whether a hiring-bar ledger can rest on Malaysian evidence. Three Codex workers (gpt-6-luna, max effort) searched primary sources on 21 October 2026, one each for official data, employer surveys and hiring practices. "Checked" marks a figure the coordinator reopened at its source that day; "worker" marks one a worker read at its source that the coordinator did not reopen.

## Verdict

Thin. All three workers reached the same verdict. Malaysia has official, updatable data on graduate underemployment and a few employer surveys on AI skills, but no public statistic on LeetCode-style tests, AI use in interviews, or portfolios versus degrees.

## What it means for us

- **The strongest Malaysian signal is underemployment, not unemployment.** In Q2 2026, 74.3% of employed tertiary-educated 15–24-year-olds and 40.7% of 25–34-year-olds worked in semi-skilled or low-skilled jobs, while ICT graduates' employment rate (92.5%) matched the average (92.3%). Graduates find work, often below their skill level.
- **Employers say they want AI skills but publish nothing on how they test for them.** Four surveys agree on the demand (65%, 62%, 70% and 89% below); no Malaysian source counts interview formats.
- **A ledger built only on Malaysian statistics would answer the students' main questions with Unknown.** The missing figures can only come from employers themselves.

### Early-career job losses in Malaysia

CERT at ISIS Malaysia analysed PERKESO Employment Insurance System cases from 2023 to 2025 ([Automation anxiety], 10 June 2026). Workers aged 25–34 had about a 25% higher association with job loss, and the result for ages 15–24 was not significant. The AI-exposure gradient was about 69% unweighted but about 3%, and not significant, when weighted by employment. The authors call these associations, not causal effects. Status: worker; the page showed the coordinator a bot check.

[Automation anxiety]: https://www.cert.isis.org.my/publications/automation-anxiety/

## Interview practices

- **Grab** ([engineering hiring]): a live coding stage that "focus[es] on data structures and algorithms" with "1–2 coding questions in real-time", then system design and culture fit. Its tips say to "keep up with emerging AI trends and be ready to discuss ethical considerations", but the page says nothing on using AI tools. Regional, not Malaysia-only. Checked.
- **Anthropic** ([candidate AI guidance], updated 10 July 2025): AI is allowed for editing applications and preparing, and disallowed in take-home and live interviews unless explicitly permitted. Global. Worker.
- No survey of Malaysian employers' interview formats or AI-use rules turned up.

[engineering hiring]: https://www.grab.careers/en/how-we-hire/engineering/
[candidate AI guidance]: https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance

## Global context

Useful for comparison, never as Malaysian evidence. All are worker-reported.

- **[Karat]** (17 December 2025), 400 engineering leaders in the US, India and China: over 60% of companies prohibit AI in technical interviews, 27% train interviewers to assess AI-enabled problem-solving, and fewer than 30% have updated interviews for AI-native workflows.
- **[HackerRank]** Developer Skills Report 2025: test invites from 2023 to 2024 rose 7% for entry-level roles, 9% for junior, 19% for senior and 22% for lead. 66% of developers prefer practical coding tasks and 56% find algorithm questions irrelevant to their jobs. The respondent count is inconsistent (13,732 on the page, 13,372 in the PDF).
- **[CodeSignal]** (25 February 2026): flagged proctored-assessment attempts rose from 16% in 2024 to 35% in 2025, and from 15% to 40% for entry-level roles. A flag is not confirmed fraud.
- **[Canaries]**, Stanford Digital Economy Lab with ADP (revised 12 August 2026): through June 2026, US employment for 22–25-year-olds in highly exposed occupations was 19% below the pace of less exposed peers, mainly through reduced hiring. The authors call it descriptive. A [dashboard][Canaries dashboard] updates monthly with downloadable data.
- **[ILO ASEAN brief]** (8 July 2026, CC BY 4.0): 22.9% of ASEAN employment was in occupations with more than minimal generative AI exposure in 2025, and 3.3% in the highest category. Youth exposure was broadly similar to adults'. No Malaysian figure on the release page.
- **[ManpowerGroup MEOS]** Q4 2026 has no Malaysian figure; its Asia Pacific and Middle East tech outlook is 41%, and in Q3, 69% of employers there would pay a premium for AI literacy.
- **[Stack Overflow survey]** 2025 had 82 Malaysian respondents out of 35,307, too few for a Malaysian figure.

## Updatable feeds

- **OpenDOSM** is the only Malaysian feed found that a product can call. Skill-related underemployment by age ([`lfs_qtr_sru_age`][OpenDOSM SRU]) is quarterly, last updated 11 August 2026 with the next update due 11 November 2026, and the labour force survey ([`lfs_month`][OpenDOSM LFS]) is monthly. Both are CC BY 4.0. The [API][OpenDOSM API] needs no key and allows 4 requests a minute ([rate limit]). Tested on 21 October 2026: `https://api.data.gov.my/opendosm/?id=lfs_qtr_sru_age&sort=-date&limit=12` returns the newest rows; without the trailing slash after `opendosm` the API answers with a 301 redirect.
- **DOSM Graduates Statistics** is annual, with the next release due 5 November 2026 (worker).
- **The Stanford dashboard** (monthly) and **ManpowerGroup MEOS** (quarterly) update regularly but are not Malaysian.
- Everything else is a one-off or annual report with no dataset to ingest.

[OpenDOSM LFS]: https://open.dosm.gov.my/data-catalogue/lfs_month
[OpenDOSM API]: https://developer.data.gov.my/static-api/opendosm
[rate limit]: https://developer.data.gov.my/rate-limit

## Gaps

- No Malaysian statistic on LeetCode-style tests, AI use in interviews, portfolios or hackathons versus degrees, junior versus senior tech vacancies, or ICT-specific graduate underemployment.
- ILMIA's MyJobProfile figures contradict themselves (Q2 2025 totals beside Q1 2024 footnotes, with zero openings for software developers), so the workers excluded them.
- No current public PERKESO or EIS series on ICT job losses turned up.
- TalentCorp's full 2026 communications and multimedia study wasn't available, so its sample and method are unknown.
