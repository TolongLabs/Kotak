# Final pitch script

The word-for-word script for Kotak's 10-minute final on 1 November 2026, slide by slide, in the shape of TolongLabs' winning [MyTakziah finals script][mytakziah]. It changes the opening of the [final plan](/docs/analyses/final-plan.md)'s run of show: we open on who we are and a hook for the room, and Aina, the sample persona, first appears on slide 3. Where the two differ, this script wins.

Status: a full draft. D7 and D8 of the demo run in kymil4's 3D space (#441), which he is still refining and which has not passed its gate yet. If it fails the gate, run them in 2D: [If the 3D space fails](#if-the-3d-space-fails). Work through [Check before rehearsal](#check-before-rehearsal) first.

Two voices: the presenter faces the judges and speaks every line; the driver works the laptop and says nothing. No one narrates clicks.

## Slide 2, the hook

**Presenter**, 0:19 to 1:13

> Hands up if you used AI at work or in class this week.

_[Pause 2 seconds. Most hands go up.]_

> Now, keep your hand up if you know which skill to learn next, how many hours it takes, and who pays.

_[Pause 2 seconds. Hands drop.]_

> That's the problem.
>
> More than half of those retrenched in Malaysia this year were professionals, managers, executives and technicians. People like many of us here.
>
> Is AI behind it? Khazanah Research Institute says: not yet, rather than not ever.
>
> But "not yet" is not a plan. You see, the money to retrain is there: RM3 billion for HRD Corp training in Budget 2026. However, what's missing is actually knowing which skill to learn first.

On screen: 51% with its source and date, the Khazanah quote, RM3 billion, and the black bar "'Not yet' is not a plan".

Why it works: the room is mostly digital workers who already worry about AI and their jobs, so the problem is theirs before any number appears. The second question names Kotak's three outputs, the skill to learn first, its hours and the funding route, so when the hands drop the room has seen the gap Kotak fills. "'Not yet' is not a plan" stays honest: it never says AI caused the job losses.

If a few hands stay up after the second question, say: "A few of you. Most of us don't." Then carry on.

## Slide 3, who it is for

**Presenter**, 1:13 to 1:40

> So who is Kotak for? Well, people in digital jobs; jobs that AI is changing now. Like Aina, our sample user, a web developer in KL.
>
> Every time a new AI model launches, she asks: is my job next?
>
> Fortunately, her manager has HRD Corp levy money, but faces one crucial question: which training do we fund first?

On screen: four cards, who uses it (developers, QA testers, UI/UX designers), the moment ("A new AI model launches. Nobody says which skill to build next."), who pays (HR and L&D managers at HRD Corp levy-paying employers) and also for (career switchers and fresh graduates), then a black bar with her manager's question, labelled a sample persona. Aina is a web developer, to match the job the demo types.

The script says "a new AI model" and names no lab, because Kotak uses Claude and Anthropic's data. Expect the question "Does Kotak update when a new model launches?" The answer is no: it updates when measured use moves in the monthly data. "A launch is news. A trend needs data. Kotak won't guess."

## Slide 4, how it works

**Presenter**, 1:40 to 2:11

> Here's how Kotak works, in three steps.
>
> One: Aina answers twelve questions about what she'd do at work.
>
> Two: Kotak checks her answers against dated public data on AI use and jobs in Malaysia. Fixed rules decide every result. AI only explains it.
>
> Three: she gets her plan. The skill to learn first, the hours, and who pays.
>
> Data decides. AI explains. Let's run Aina's check-up, live.

_[Advance to slide 5.]_

On screen: the three steps as one picture, built from Aina's real check-up. Answer: her first question, on technical documentation, with each option's hidden score. Decide: fixed rules turn her answers into six skill levels on a radar, against the dashed line of what her job needs by 2027. Act: learn technical documentation first, 47.5 hours to the next level, the HRD Corp route, and her fit from 47% today to 100% after the full plan. Say none of these numbers here: the demo shows them live. Don't read the six stages either: the presenter names each one in the demo ("Stage 3 of 6: matching"), so the judges can tick them off.

Step three, like the hook's second question, names Kotak's three outputs: the skill to learn first, the hours and who pays.

## Slide 7, technical architecture

**Presenter**, 7:35 to 8:30

> Under the hood: two halves, one rulebook.
>
> First, the engine, at build time. Bun pipelines turn the Economic Index, O*NET and DOSM into dated monthly snapshots. Each skill's AI trend resolves Malaysia-first, down six O*NET levels, before any global figure.
>
> Then followed by RunPod GPU batch, Qwen on vLLM, drafted skills for all 923 jobs in 13 minutes, and validators reject any task that isn't the job's own.
>
> Finally the product. One TypeScript rulebook runs in your browser and on our edge API, so every result is reproducible. Our AI layer - Claude Haiku, only explains: a verifier keeps its text only if it names the exact verdict, cites each source by date, and adds no number of its own.

On screen: the architecture slide, "Two Halves, One Rulebook, No Guessing." Point to the engine half (left) on "First, the engine", to GPU Batch and Validators on "A RunPod GPU batch", then to the product half (right) on "Then the product". Every name said aloud is on the slide; Kopi's voice and avatar (Haiku 5.5, ElevenLabs, Live2D) and the job-ad pipeline are on the slide only.

Each technical line has its source in the code: the Malaysia-first lookup in `packages/shared/src/rules/resolve.ts`, the task validator in `packages/shared/src/corners/validate.ts`, the Durable Object in `apps/api/src/worker.ts`, and the explanation verifier in `apps/api/src/ai/verifier.ts`. If a judge asks who wrote the what-would-you-do questions: Claude Sonnet and GPT, as coding agents in the team's own sessions at build time, not inside the product. A script checked every question (four answers scored 1 to 4 exactly once, no giveaway words, task IDs from the job), and Claude Sonnet, GPT and Gemini reviewed the demo jobs and a sample of the rest ([technology declaration](/docs/tech-declaration.md)).
