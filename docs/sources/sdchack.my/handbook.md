## 1. Event overview

- **Event name**: SDC: Sustainably Developing Code Hackathon Competition
- **Organizer**: XMUM AI Club
- **Collaborator**: XMUM Rotaract Club
- **Date**: Online Week — October 19th – October 30th, 2026. Physical Hackathon Starts — October 31st. Pitching Day — November 1st.
- **Team size**: 1–5 members per team

## 2. Objective

### 2.1. To bridge the gap between theory and application

To provide a structured environment where students can translate academic knowledge into practical, deployed applications through intensive workshops and mentorship.

### 2.2. To solve real-world challenges via AI

The event features two to three specific domains focused on developing AI solutions to any problem that aligns with any SDG ranging from 1 - 17.

### 2.3. To foster inter-university & industry collaboration

To connect the top tech talent from major universities with industry mentors and sponsoring companies, creating a "Tech Festival" atmosphere promoting networking and future employment opportunities.

### 2.4. To cultivate student ingenuity and complex problem-solving

To empower students to apply their ingenuity, creativity, and problem-solving capabilities to meaningful, real-world challenges. By moving beyond traditional case studies, participants are required to deploy design-thinking methodologies and critical analysis to ensure their solutions are not only technologically viable but also socially or economically impactful.

## 4. Participant eligibility

The SDC: Sustainably Developing Code Hackathon Competition is open to students who are interested in developing AI-based solutions that address real-world challenges aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

### 4.1. General eligibility

4.1.1. Participation is open to students who are officially enrolled in any degree in any recognised Malaysian higher education institutions.

4.1.2. Eligible participants include students currently pursuing:

- a. Foundation programmes;
- b. Diploma programmes; or
- c. Bachelor's Degree programmes.

4.1.3. Each participant may only be registered under one team throughout the competition.

4.1.4. Participants must remain enrolled as students throughout the duration of the Competition.

4.1.5. Participants must be available to participate in the required online sessions, preliminary round, and, if shortlisted, the final round.

4.1.6. Participants must agree to follow all competition rules, submission requirements, judging procedures, deadlines, and instructions given by the organizing committee.

4.1.7. Committees and any other parties directly involved in organizing, planning, managing, judging, mentoring, or administering the event are not allowed to participate as contestants.

4.1.8. The Organiser reserves the right to reject any registration that fails the eligibility verification process.

### 4.2. Team eligibility

4.2.1. The registration fee is RM40 per team for Early Bird registration and RM50 per team for Normal registration.

4.2.2. Each team is required to pay a RM25 commitment fee as part of the registration process.

4.2.3. A team may consist of 1 to 5 members.

4.2.4. A team may consist of members from the same university or different universities.

4.2.5. All participants, regardless of their university or institution, must comply with Xiamen University Malaysia’s campus rules, regulations, and disciplinary guidelines during the physical phase of the event.

4.2.6. Participants are expected to respect all campus facilities, event venues, university staff, committee members, and other participants while on-site.

4.2.7. Any misconduct, violation of campus rules, or failure to follow instructions from the organizing committee may result in disciplinary action, penalties, or disqualification.

4.2.8. Each team must appoint one team leader as the main point of contact with the organizing committee.

4.2.9. The team leader is responsible for registration, receiving official announcements, coordinating team communication, and submitting the team’s final deliverables.

4.2.10. All team members are required to join the official Discord server. All important announcements, updates, and instructions will be communicated through the server.

4.2.11. No changes to team members are allowed after registration has been completed, nor after the event has officially started.

4.2.12. Unregistered individuals are not allowed to contribute substantially to a team’s project, prototype, presentation, documentation, or final submission.

4.2.13. Teams that fail to meet the team composition requirements may be considered ineligible for judging.

### 4.3. Commitment fee and refund policy

4.3.1. Each team is required to pay a RM25 commitment fee as part of the registration process.

4.3.2. The commitment fee is refundable only after the event, subject to the team’s fulfilment of the submission requirements.

4.3.3. To be eligible for a refund, the team must submit a valid preliminary submission, which includes:

- 4.3.3.1. A working prototype;
- 4.3.3.2. A complete code repository;
- 4.3.3.3. All required documentation stated by the organizing committee.

4.3.4. Teams that do not submit any preliminary submission will not be eligible for a refund.

4.3.5. Teams that submit incomplete, inaccessible, invalid, or insufficient work will not be eligible for a refund.

4.3.6. Shortlisted teams that fail to attend the physical event without providing at least 48 hours’ prior notice will have their RM25 commitment fee permanently forfeited, as physical space, event resources, and logistical arrangements have been reserved for them.

4.3.7. The organizing committee reserves the right to verify whether a team’s preliminary submission meets the minimum refund requirements.

4.3.8. Refunds will be processed after the event, subject to verification by the organizing committee.

4.3.9. The exact refund timeline will be confirmed and announced by the organizing committee.

### 4.4. Finalist eligibility

4.4.1. Successfully complete the online preliminary phase.

4.4.2. Submit all required deliverables before the stated deadline.

4.4.3. Be shortlisted by the judges or organizing committee based on the preliminary round evaluation.

4.4.4. Finalist teams must confirm their participation within the deadline given by the organizing committee. Failure to confirm within the stated deadline may result in the finalist slot being offered to another eligible team based on the shortlist.

4.4.5. Attend the physical final round at Xiamen University Malaysia according to the attendance requirement set by the organizing committee.

## 7. Problem statement / challenge brief

### 7.1. Overview

The SDC Hackathon challenges participants to design and build AI-based solutions that address real-world problems aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Each team will be randomly assigned a domain to compete in. Every domain revolves around a specific SDG (1–17). Participants are free to identify the specific problem within their assigned domain's scope, as long as the solution demonstrates clear alignment with at least one SDG.

### 7.2. Competition domains

The SDC Hackathon 2026 features two competition domains, each presented in partnership with an organization.

### 7.3. Domain selection

Detailed problem statements for each domain, including specific challenge briefs, sponsor-provided resources, and domain-specific evaluation notes, will be released to registered teams via the Participant Handbook following the close of registration and formally announced on 19 October 2026 during the Participants Orientation/Problem Statement Announcement Day.

7.3.1. Teams will be randomly assigned a domain during registration; domains are not self-selected.

7.3.2. Domain assignments are locked upon registration. No switching of domains is permitted at any point after assignment, including once the preliminary round has started.

7.3.3. As domains are assigned randomly, there is no cap on the number of teams per domain.

### 7.4. Scope and constraints

7.4.1. Solutions must be developed primarily during the official hacking period; any pre-existing work must be declared per Section 8.2.

7.4.2. Teams are free to define their own specific problem angle within their selected domain, provided it aligns with the domain's sponsor-defined brief once released.

7.4.3. Solutions must include a working technical prototype, a live presentation/pitch, and a functional user interface; all three are required, and a submission is considered incomplete if any one is missing. A polished pitch cannot substitute for a working build, and a working build without a presentable interface or a pitch to explain it is equally insufficient.

## 8. Allowed and prohibited tools

### 8.1. Use of external tools, APIs, and cloud services

8.1.1. Teams may use free-tier, open-source, sponsor-provided, or paid APIs, cloud services, and development tools during the hackathon. Unless otherwise stated by the organizing committee, all costs incurred from paid tools, APIs, cloud platforms, storage, deployment, or third-party services must be covered by the team itself.

8.1.2. If the organizing committee or sponsors provide free API keys, cloud credits, or other technical resources, teams must use them according to the stated limits, terms, and instructions. Any usage beyond the provided quota will be the responsibility of the team.

8.1.3. Teams must clearly declare any major APIs, cloud services, paid tools, sponsor-provided resources, or third-party platforms used in their project submission or documentation.

### 8.2. Use of pre-existing code and materials

8.2.1. Teams that intend to build on any pre-existing code, frameworks, models, templates, or project materials created before the hackathon must declare them during registration and obtain approval from the organizing committee; undeclared pre-existing work may be considered a violation of the originality rules and may result in point deduction under the innovation creativity criteria.

### 8.3. Third-party platform outage contingency

8.3.1. In the event of a major third-party platform outage affecting essential services such as GitHub, AWS, OpenAI, Replit, or other approved tools during the hacking period, the organizing committee will assess the situation and may extend the submission deadline or apply alternative offline evaluation criteria where necessary. Any official decision or adjustment will be announced immediately through the official Discord server.

### 8.4. Declaration requirement

8.4.1. Teams must declare all major external tools, APIs, cloud services, AI tools, datasets, models, sponsor-provided resources, and pre-existing materials used in their project submission or documentation.

8.4.2. Failure to declare required resources may result in point deduction, disqualification, or other action deemed appropriate by the organizing committee.

### 8.5. Use of AI tools in development

8.5.1. Teams are permitted to use AI tools (including but not limited to code assistants, LLMs, and AI-powered development platforms) to support their project's development, provided their use complies with the declaration requirements in Section 8.4. Code, content, or assets generated by AI tools during the official hacking period, in direct response to the team's own prompts, are not considered 'pre-existing work' under Section 8.2 and do not require separate declaration/approval; they are covered under the AI-use rules in 8.5 instead.

8.5.2. AI tools may be used to assist with tasks such as code generation, debugging, documentation, and design support. However, the core problem understanding, solution design, and technical decision-making must originate from the team itself.

8.5.3. Teams must be able to explain and justify all major components of their submitted project, including any AI-generated code, design, or content during judging or verification. Inability to explain a substantial part of the submission may be treated as a sign of possible misrepresentation.

8.5.4. Submitting a project that is substantially AI-generated with minimal original input, understanding, or modification by the team may be considered a violation of the originality requirements and may result in point deduction or disqualification, at the discretion of the organizing committee.

8.5.5. Further guidance on acceptable AI usage, including domain-specific restrictions if any, will be provided in the Participant Handbook.

8.5.6. To help teams and judges apply a consistent standard, AI usage is acceptable when:

- The team can articulate why they made each major technical or design decision, independent of what the AI suggested.
- AI-generated code/content was reviewed, modified, or adapted by the team, not used unedited wholesale.
- The core problem framing and solution approach originated from the team's own understanding of the SDG/domain brief, not from an AI-generated project idea.

8.5.7. During judging or verification, mentors/judges may ask any team member to explain any part of the submission, including AI-assisted portions. A team that cannot explain the logic, structure, or purpose of a substantial component (e.g., a core algorithm, key architecture decision, or main feature) may be flagged under Section 8.5.4 for lacking original input, regardless of whether that component was hand-written or AI-assisted.

## 9. Prizes and awards

The SDC Hackathon offers a total prize pool of up to RM16,000, awarded across the competition domains to recognize teams that demonstrate strong technical execution, innovation, real-world impact, and effective problem-solving through AI-based solutions aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals. E-certificates will also be provided to all participants, finalists, and winners as official recognition of their involvement and achievements in the hackathon.

## 10. Mentorship / support

### 10.1. Mentorship availability

10.1.1. Mentorship support will be provided both during preliminary round and final round of the SDC Hackathon to guide teams in improving their solutions, technical implementation, and final presentation.

10.1.2. Online mentorship will be available during the Phase 1 Online Preliminary Round, 19–25 October 2026.

10.1.3. Physical mentorship will be available during the Final Phase Physical Hackathon, 31 October 2026, at Xiamen University Malaysia.

### 10.2. Mentor slot booking

10.2.1. Mentor slots will be arranged on a first-come, first-served (FCFS) basis, subject to mentor availability.

10.2.2. Teams must request or book mentorship through the official process announced by the organizing committee through the website.

10.2.3. Teams are encouraged to prepare specific questions before meeting mentors to make the session more effective.

### 10.3. What mentors can help with

10.3.1. Mentors may provide feedback on the team’s problem understanding, solution direction, and overall project feasibility.

10.3.2. Mentors may guide teams on technical approach, AI integration, system design, deployment direction, and debugging strategy.

10.3.3. Mentors may give advice on pitch structure, prototype demonstration, user experience, impact explanation, and SDG alignment.

10.3.4. Mentors may help teams identify weaknesses in their project and suggest areas for improvement.

### 10.4. What mentor cannot do

10.4.1. Mentors are not allowed to directly build, code, design, write documentation, or complete any part of the project for a team.

10.4.2. Mentors are not allowed to make final project decisions on behalf of a team.

10.4.3. Mentors are not allowed to give unfair advantages, confidential judging information, or private instructions that are not available to other teams.

10.4.4. Mentors are not responsible for ensuring that a team’s project works, is submitted correctly, or meets the judging requirements.

Participants may use suitable public datasets, self-collected data, open-source resources, or other permitted data sources, as long as they comply with the competition rules and properly acknowledge the source where required.

Participants are advised to refer to the official domain-specific folder or guideline for the latest updates regarding dataset requirements, restrictions, or recommended resources.

### 12.30. Is there any specific programming language required?

No specific programming language is required. Participants may use any programming language, framework, library, or development tool that they are comfortable with, as long as it is suitable for building their proposed solution.

Participants are encouraged to use technologies that help them develop a functional and presentable prototype or minimum viable product. Teams should also be prepared to explain their technical choices, implementation process, and how their solution works during submission or pitching.
