## Cover and submission rules

The first page reads "SDC HACKATHON 2026", "Phase 2: Final Project & Prototype Report", "Hosted by Xiamen University Malaysia (XMUM) AI Club" and "Submission Deadline: 1 November 2026". Unlike the Phase 1 template it has no designed cover image. The instructions box says, verbatim:

> Instructions: Complete every field below and submit as a single PDF document named “SDC2026_TeamName_Final.pdf” together with your pitch deck and a working deployment link. Answers must comply with the word limits stated for each open-ended question. Section 3 requires concrete, verifiable evidence. Unsupported claims will not be credited by judges.

What that means in practice:

- **File**: one PDF named `SDC2026_TeamName_Final.pdf`, with your team name in place of `TeamName`. The template is only shared as a `.docx`; the organisers' 23 October announcement says to convert to PDF and not submit in Word format.
- **Submitted with it**: the pitch deck, as a flattened PDF per the [checklist](#6-final-submission-checklist), and a working deployment link.
- **Deadline**: 1 November 2026. The template gives no time of day.
- **Word limits**: 250 words for section 2, 300 words for 3.2 and 250 words for section 5. No limit is stated for 3.1, section 4 or 4.1.
- **Evidence**: the box points to section 3 for "concrete, verifiable evidence", but the evidence table and the proof are in [section 4](#4-environmental--sdg-impact-verification).

Short fields have a blank answer line, and open-ended questions have a blank answer box.

## 3. Upgraded product & technical execution

### 3.1. Final system architecture

Provide a detailed description (and, if possible, an attached technical diagram) of your fully built system. Explain how your application integrates GenAI APIs, database structures, and backend/frontend frameworks into one unified solution.

Answer space: one blank box. No word limit is stated.

### 3.2. Implementation & hackathon deliverables

Describe what was successfully built, integrated, and deployed during the 48-hour physical sprint. Highlight any specific problem-solving steps your team took to pivot or improve the project from the Phase 1 preliminary design.

**[Maximum 300 words]**

Answer space: one blank box.

## 4. Environmental & SDG impact verification

Validate the real-world impact of your built solution. Show concrete data that validate your environmental or social value proposition, compared against the baseline and KPI defined in previous phase submission.

The data and evidence you provide in this section will be used to assess **SDG Impact, Technical Execution and Innovation scores.** Vague claims or missing verification will weaken your score across all three categories.

A two-column table headed `Metric Category` and `Your Response`. Each row's left cell holds the metric name with a prompt, an example or both; the `Your Response` cell is blank. The four rows, in order:

1.  **Target SDG Metric Achievement**: Direct comparison between your Phase 1 baseline and your Phase 2 finalized prototype numbers
    - e.g., “Achieved a 24.5% reduction in computational execution overhead against the 20% initial target.”
1.  **Verification Evidence Method**: How was this data verified?
    - e.g., codebase algorithmic profiling logs, hardware power-meter simulation readings, or database transaction counters.
1.  **Socio-Economic / Civic Value**: How does your deployed application translate into a scalable, real-world solution capable of being supported by civic or corporate partners?
1.  **Deployment & Commercial Feasibility**
    - e.g., modeled as an API service plugin designed for easy integration into existing corporate or institutional systems.

### 4.1. Mathematical proof / code logic snippet

Headed "(Optional, but holds bonus marks)".

Paste the specific code section, mathematical formula, simulation log output, or calculation logic that proves the impact claims made above.

This proof is graded across three categories at once: **Impact and Feasibility** (does the math support your claim), **Technical Execution** (does it reflect what you actually built), as well as **Innovation and Creativity** (is the approach original).] Do not leave this section generic or copied from Phase 1.

Answer space: one blank box. No word limit is stated.

## Differences from the Phase 1 template

Against the [Phase 1 proposal template](submission-template.md):

- **Section 1** drops Selected Domain, Selected SDG Track, University Affiliation(s) and Number of Team Members, and adds Final Live Deployment URL and Codebase Repository URL.
- **Sections 2 to 5** are new. The problem context and proposed solution become one unprompted 250-word description; the planned tech stack becomes the built architecture; and the impact baseline table becomes a verification table against that baseline, with an optional bonus proof and a new market viability section.
- **Section 6** replaces the eligibility checklist with deployment, repository, pitch deck and completeness checks.
- **Section 7** keeps the three IP questions and adds the "No change from Phase 1 declaration" option to each. The 7.2 table loses its five blank rows, and 7.1 and 7.3 lose their answer boxes.
- **Section 8**, the declaration, now confirms that "the deployed application and data presented above are the original work of the team, completed within the official SDC 2026 hackathon period", where Phase 1 confirmed the information was accurate and the project would be original work.
