# Baki

Baki, Malay for remainder, finds the work a company's AI support bot leaves behind and turns it into new roles for the people who used to answer those queries. It clusters the conversations the bot couldn't resolve and re-runs a newer model over them to see which clusters are durable human work. It then matches each agent to that work, with a pathway to get there. See the [ranking](0000-ranking.md) for its place.

## User and moment

- **Primary user**: the head of customer service at a Malaysian company or shared-services centre that runs an AI support bot. Each month's report shows the bot resolving more, and someone must decide what the human agents do next.
- **Second user**: each agent, who sees which of the remaining work suits them and owns the plan.
- **Why now**: IKEA's bot Billie went from assisting 47% of customers who used it in its first two years to 74%. Ingka Group retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff "to handle more complicated customer queries or to work as interior design sales advisors" ([Fortune, 30 July 2026][fortune]). IKEA found that new work by hand. Baki is meant to find it in any company's own logs.

[fortune]: https://fortune.com/2026/07/30/ikea-ai-workforce-reskilling-jobs-billie-chatbot-global-500/

## How it works

1.  **Logs in.** The company exports a month of bot conversations with their outcome: resolved, escalated or abandoned. The demo uses synthetic logs, labelled as such.
2.  **Cluster the remainder.** Unresolved and escalated conversations are embedded with an open model on a RunPod GPU and clustered. Each cluster is mapped only to real O\*NET tasks and occupations, never to an invented role, and sized by volume and month-on-month trend.
3.  **Forecast.** A newer model is re-run over the same conversations. Clusters it now resolves are "next to go"; clusters it still can't resolve are durable human work. The [Anthropic Economic Index][aei]'s usage for customer-service occupations gives the public, dated trend beside the company's own.
4.  **Match.** Each agent is ranked against the durable clusters by skill overlap with the mapped occupations, with the score broken down and the gap named.
5.  **Plan.** Free modules with real durations from the Microsoft Learn catalogue, each step ending in a small piece of work rather than a quiz, plus who pays: the HRD Corp levy for employed staff.
6.  **SDG.** Each cluster is tagged by its subject, such as repair and reuse questions to target 12.5 on waste, alongside 8.5 and 4.4 for the move itself, so the tag follows the actual work.

[aei]: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex

## The six must-haves

| Must-have             | How Baki meets it                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Skills profile intake | Agent profiles from a short questionnaire, with sample profiles                                               |
| AI-trend signal layer | Dated model releases re-run on the logs, plus dated Economic Index snapshots for customer-service occupations |
| Future-role matching  | Agents ranked against durable clusters mapped to real occupations, with the score broken down                 |
| Reskilling pathway    | Ordered free modules with durations and a finish date                                                         |
| SDG mapping           | Tags from each cluster's subject, plus the targets for the move itself                                        |
| Handling uncertainty  | Two scenarios: resolution keeps rising at the observed pace, or it plateaus                                   |

## Why it stands out

Our [field simulation][field] holds 16 HR redeployment planners, but each starts from a public exposure score for job titles. Baki starts from the company's own evidence of what its AI can't do. One of two checkers matched it to a single field idea, on outcome rather than mechanism. It also gives a "trend signal" that the head of service already watches: the bot's resolution rate.

[field]: /docs/research/field-simulation.md

## Why people would use it

- **The bot report is monthly**, so the remainder analysis has a built-in rhythm.
- **It answers a real decision**: who to retrain and for what, before the next headcount review.
- **It finds revenue as well as cost**: IKEA's remainder became a design-sales service.

## Hardest risk and fallback

- **Synthetic logs can look like invented evidence.** Label them on every screen, publish the generator, and state that a real deployment uses the company's own logs.
- **Clusters can be mapped to the wrong occupation.** Show the top three O\*NET candidates with similarity scores, and let a human pick.
- **The public trend layer is thin for one sector.** Pair the Economic Index's customer-service rows with the model re-run, and say which is which.

## Scores

Scores out of 5, from round 9 (ours) and the round 10 panel, three other Claude models judging blind in persona: Claude Sonnet 5.5 as the sponsor, Claude Fable 5.1 as a government official and Claude Haiku 5.5 as a technical mentor. The [ranking](0000-ranking.md#scoring-matrix) explains the method.

| Criterion            | Weight | Ours | Sponsor judge | Government judge | Technical judge |
| -------------------- | ------ | ---- | ------------- | ---------------- | --------------- |
| Innovation           | 30%    | 5    | 4             | 4                | 4               |
| Problem-solution fit | 25%    | 4    | 3             | 3                | 3               |
| Feasibility          | 25%    | 4    | 3             | 4                | 3               |
| Clarity              | 20%    | 5    | 4             | 4                | 4               |
| Actual use           |        | 4    | 3             | 4                | 3               |

- **Rubric**: 90 from us and 71.7 from the panel, 80.8 on average.
- **Uniqueness**: 4: one checker matched it to one HR planner on outcome.
- **Composite**: 79.6, rank 3 of 5 finalists. All three judges picked Dividen as the winner.

What the judges attacked, and the fix we adopt:

- **The forecast is circular on synthetic tickets** (sponsor and technical judges). Backtest it on an earlier pair of model releases and show the hit rate, and check whether a public customer-support dataset with a usable licence can replace the synthetic logs.
- **Matching stays inside customer service** (government judge). Rank adjacent O\*NET occupations, such as sales, quality assurance and training, not only clusters of the same job.
- **Ticket-subject SDG tags look decorative** (government and sponsor judges). Take SDG tags from the destination role, and keep the ticket subject only as context.
- **Only firms with a bot and logs qualify** (government judge). Say so; it is a narrow, sharp slice by design.
